# Reformer Class Plan — Full Body, Leg Focus

**Duration:** ~45 min · **Level:** Mixed / all levels · **Style:** Group reformer

**Props:** Straps (feet & hands) · Small ball · Short box (cooldown option)

**Setup note:** Footbar stays **up** until Section 4, then comes down once for the core/upper block. Keep the small ball within reach from the start; grab the short box only for the cooldown.

---

## How to read this plan

Each section below lists the **exercises**, the **variations & cues** (how to run it and what to watch for), and the **spring options**.

Because this is a mixed-level group, every block gives you a **base setting** plus how to **make it easier** (regress) and **harder** (advance) — so you can meet everyone in the same room without changing the choreography.

**Spring key:** 🔴 **R** = red (heavy) · 🔵 **B** = blue (medium) · 🟡 **Y** = yellow (light). Combinations read left to right, e.g. `RRR` = three reds, `RB` = red + blue.

> **Spring colours vary by brand.** This plan uses the R / B / Y convention from the Barre Body repertoire. If your studio uses a different colour system (e.g. Balanced Body green/blue/red/yellow), translate by **resistance level** — heavy / medium / light — not by colour name.

**The leg arc:** three back-to-back leg blocks (~25 min) move **heavy → light** and **bilateral → unilateral**: footwork (both legs, heavy) → straps (long levers, moderate) → standing (single leg, light). Section 4 then restores core and upper body so the class still reads full-body.

## Warm-up / Mobility · ~5 min

**Purpose:** Connect breath and powerhouse, mobilise the spine, and gently wake the legs before loading them.

**Exercises**

1. **Breathwork + Cat/Cow on the carriage** — lateral thoracic breathing, then big breath-led spinal articulation. No counting — move with the breath.
2. **Bridge + ball squeeze** — ball between the thighs; articulate the pelvis up and down, squeezing the ball on the way up to switch on glutes and inner thighs. Add small heel lifts at the top to preview calf work. ~5–8 reps.
3. **Double leg press (easy tempo)** — parallel heels on the footbar, flowing tempo to build heat and hint at what's coming.

**Variations & cues**

- Keep Cat/Cow large and unhurried — this sets the breath rhythm for the whole class.
- In the bridge, cue *"peel the spine off the carriage one vertebra at a time — a string of pearls."*
- Ball squeeze is a **connection cue**, not a max effort — *"gently hug the ball, feel your inner thighs answer."*

**Spring options**

| Exercise | Base | Notes |
|----------|------|-------|
| Cat/Cow | �🔴R | Same setting as the rest of warm-up — no spring change to start the class. |
| Bridge + ball | 🔴🔴R | Enough load to feel the glutes without gripping. |
| Double leg press | 🔴🔴R | Warm-up load — heavier reds come in Section 1. |

## Section 1 · Footwork Foundations · ~9 min

**Purpose:** Bilateral, heavier leg work on the footbar — the strongest, most stable position, so we load the legs most here.

**Exercises**

1. **Double leg press series** — heels → toes/prehensile → wide/turnout to bias calves, quads, and adductors differently.
2. **Heel raises** — slow eccentric lower, no bouncing.
3. **Prances** — roll one heel under as the opposite knee bends; rhythm and ankle mobility.
4. **Single leg press** — free leg at tabletop; keep the pelvis level.

**Variations & cues**

- Cue the press from *"the whole foot, not just the ball"* and a controlled return — *"resist the carriage home."*
- Heel raises: *"lift to the highest point over the big toe, lower with control below the bar line."*
- Single leg press is where the pelvis tends to tip — *"keep both hip bones facing the ceiling, level like a tabletop."*

**Spring options**

| Level | Setting | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| Base (bilateral) | 🔴🔴🔴R | Heaviest block of the class — stable position supports the load. |
| Single leg press | 🔴🔴R | Drop one red so a single leg isn't overloaded. |
| Easier | 🔴🔴R | Keeps knees comfortable; still plenty of work. |
| Advance | Keep 🔴🔴🔴R for single leg, slow the tempo | More load + longer time under tension. |

## Section 2 · Feet in Straps · ~8 min

**Purpose:** Long-lever hamstring and hip work with the pelvis supported. Same spring family as footwork, so **no big spring change** flowing in — keeps the class moving.

**Exercises**

1. **Hamstring press** — heels in straps, legs long; drive from the back of the legs.
2. **Leg circles** — both directions; circles no bigger than the pelvis can stay still.
3. **Frog press** — heels together in a small "V"; press out and squeeze the adductors on the return.
4. **Ball option:** ball between the ankles during frog/leg circles for extra inner-thigh feedback.
5. **Progression (strong groups):** finish with **Short Spine** — headrest down, roll the hips up and articulate down through the spine.

**Variations & cues**

- *"Pelvis stays heavy and quiet — the legs move, the torso doesn't."*
- Leg circles: *"draw the circle from the hip, not the foot; only as big as your centre stays still."*
- Frog: *"press to straight legs on a diagonal, then bend and open the knees like a frog — squeeze the ball on the way in."*

**Spring options**

| Level | Setting | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| Base | 🔴🔴R | Same as end of footwork — smooth transition, no reset. |
| Easier | 🔴R + small range | Lighter load, shorter levers protect the lower back. |
| Advance | 🔴🔴R + Short Spine finisher | Adds a spinal articulation challenge. |

> **Watch-point:** if anyone's lower back lifts or arches, shorten the lever (bend the knees) or reduce the range rather than adding spring.

## Section 3 · Standing Lower Body · ~8 min

**Purpose:** Unilateral, lighter work that adds balance and glute demand. Lighter springs mean **more stability challenge**, not less work. For mixed-level groups, drop the side-splits finisher below to protect time for Section 4's core/upper work.

**Exercises**

1. **Squats** (parallel) → **Sumo squat** (wide turnout) — quads into adductors and glutes.
2. **Scooter** — standing on the platform, other foot on the carriage; stability-focused.
3. **Flying lunge** — paired straight after scooter (stability → mobility pairing).
4. **Lateral lunge** — control the carriage home, no slamming.
5. **Optional finisher (strong groups only — cut for mixed-level classes):** **Standing side splits**.

**Setup & orientation**

Footbar stays **up** for this whole block and acts as the hand support. A heavier spring pins the carriage against the stopper (more stable); a lighter spring lets it travel (more stability demand).

| Exercise | You are facing | Base foot | Working foot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Squats (parallel) | The footbar, standing **on the carriage** | Both feet on the carriage, hip-width, parallel, heels near the shoulder blocks | Both |
| Sumo squat | The footbar, standing **on the carriage** | Both feet wide, outside the blocks, toes turned out ~45 degrees | Both |
| Scooter | The footbar | Standing platform | Carriage, presses straight back |
| Flying lunge | The footbar | Standing platform (front foot) | Carriage (back foot), slides into a long lunge |
| Lateral lunge | **Side-on**, facing the long side of the reformer | Standing platform | Carriage, abducts out to the side |
| Standing side splits | **Side-on**, facing the long side | Standing platform | Carriage, both legs stay long |

- **Squat vs sumo:** parallel drives the quads; turnout shifts load into adductors and glutes. Knees track over the second toe in both. Footbar is a balance point, not something to pull on.
- **Scooter vs flying lunge:** same setup, different intent. Scooter is a short push with the hips locked square (stability). Flying lunge takes a long range and a deep front-knee bend (mobility).
- **Lateral lunge vs side splits:** lateral lunge is asymmetric, the standing knee bends and the hips sit back over it. Side splits is symmetrical with both legs long, which is why it reads as the finisher.
- **The 90-degree turn** into lateral lunge is the awkward moment. Script it: *"step down, turn to face me, standing foot back onto the platform, outside foot to the carriage."* Safer than pivoting on a live carriage.

**Variations & cues**

- Layer per side and swap with minimal fuss to keep flow — *"we'll do the whole set on the right, then match it on the left."*
- Scooter: *"square the hips forward, glute of the standing leg switched on."*
- Lateral lunge: *"press the carriage out with control, resist it all the way home."*

**Spring options**

| Level | Setting | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| Base | 🔴R | One spring choice for the whole block — strength & support. |
| Advance | 🔵B | Lighter = **more** stability demand and range. |
| Easier / less confident | 🔴R + hand on footbar | Extra support point for balance. |

> **One spring for the block.** Choose the setting per person at the start of the section and leave it — this block is about flow and balance, not spring changes.

## Section 4 · Core + Upper Body · ~10 min

**Purpose:** Restore full-body balance after all the leg work. Posterior-chain and core work counters the pressing bias. **Footbar comes down here** — take one breath cycle while it lowers so the block doesn't start immediately on lunge-fatigued hip flexors.

**Exercises**

1. **Seated row** (facing the risers) — posterior chain balance after all the pressing, run first on the same red spring carried over from Section 3. *Short box option:* sit on the box for a taller, more supported row.
2. **Full plank** → **Front knee tuck** (option: **Pike**) — a layered progression; legs stay engaged so the leg theme carries through.
3. **Kneeling biceps curl** — hold the knots/handles; scapula drawn down.

**Variations & cues**

- Seated row: *"melt the shoulder blades down into your back pockets before you pull."*
- Plank → knee tuck → pike as one ladder: everyone starts at plank, individuals climb as far as they own it.
- *"Shoulders stacked over wrists, ribs knitted, one long line from crown to heels."*

**Spring options**

| Exercise | Setting | Notes |
|----------|---------|-------|
| Seated row | 🔴R | Same red as Section 3 — no spring change on entry to this block. |
| Plank / knee tuck / pike | 🔵B | **Lighter to advance** — less spring = carriage moves more, more core demand. |
| Biceps curl | 🔵B | Control the eccentric (lowering) phase; hold the knots to advance. Same blue as plank — no change into the last exercise. |

---

## Cooldown / Stretch · ~5 min

**Purpose:** Open the hip flexors and hamstrings that just worked hard, lengthen the side body, and settle the breath.

**Exercises**

1. **Hip opener + quad stretch** — long holds for the hip flexors that just lunged.
2. **Hamstring stretch** — hands on the footbar (or heel in strap), hinge from the hips with a long spine.
3. **Mermaid** — side-body opening; kneeling on the carriage or seated on the **short box** for a taller stretch. Finish with a few slow seated breaths.

**Variations & cues**

- *"Breathe into the stretch — exhale to soften a little deeper each time."*
- Hamstring stretch: *"hinge from the hips, keep the spine long rather than rounding to reach."*
- Mermaid: *"reach up and over, feel the ribs open on the stretched side."*

**Spring options**

| Setting | Why |
|---------|-----|
| 🔵B | Medium tension for **supported** stretching — the spring is support, not resistance. |

## Teaching Notes

**Spring flow.** The full sequence runs **🔴🔴R (warm-up) → 🔴🔴🔴R → 🔴🔴R → 🔴R → 🔴R (row) → 🔵B (plank) → 🔵B (curl) → 🔵B (cooldown)** — four spring changes end to end, down from seven in the earlier draft. The one remaining change into Section 4 (red → blue) now happens once, between the row and the plank, instead of bouncing back and forth mid-block.

**Leg dose.** Three consecutive leg blocks (~25 min): footwork (bilateral, heavy) → straps (long levers, moderate) → standing (unilateral, light). Heavy-to-light and bilateral-before-unilateral is deliberate — it fatigues the legs safely and progressively; standing is trimmed to 8 min (side splits cut for mixed-level groups) to free up time for Section 4's core/upper block.

**Balance.** Section 4 restores core and upper body so the class reads full-body; the seated row specifically counters all the pressing.

**Mixed-level strategy.** Choreography stays the same for everyone — you differentiate with **spring** and **range/tempo**, not different exercises. Set expectations early: *"the movement is the same for all of us today; your spring and your range are yours to choose."*

**Watch-points.**
- Pelvis level in single-leg press and standing work.
- Leg circles only as big as the pelvis stays quiet.
- Control the carriage *home* in all lunge and press work — no slamming.
- Scapula depressed (shoulders out of ears) in rows, curls, and plank.

**Modifications & contraindications.**
- **Pregnancy (past first trimester):** avoid staying supine more than a few minutes — swap footwork/straps for side-lying leg work, use an incline/wedge, and skip the pike. No deep spinal flexion under load.
- **Lower-back sensitivity:** shorten levers (bend knees) and reduce range in straps before adding spring; imprint the spine in supine work.
- **Knee sensitivity:** partial range in footwork and standing work; keep the knee tracking over the toes.
- **Wrist/shoulder sensitivity:** offer forearm plank; keep loads light in curls and rows.
- **Balance / newer clients:** keep a hand on the footbar in standing work and choose the heavier (more supportive) spring.
