# Reformer Class Plan — Full Body, Leg Focus (v3 · The Circle)

**Duration:** ~46 min · **Level:** Mixed / all levels · **Style:** Group reformer

**Props:** Small ball (all class) · Straps, hands only (final block) · Short box (final block + cooldown)

**Setup note:** Footbar stays **up** for the first 30 minutes and comes **down once**, at the same moment as the last spring change. The ball lives on the floor by the shoulder blocks from the start. The box comes out **once**, for the seated arm work, and stays out for the mermaid.

> **This is v3.** The squat section is cut. The leg series is now split around a mid-class core block — right side → core → left side → pike — so the class runs as a circle. Teaser is out. See *What changed and why* at the end.

---

## How to read this plan

Each section lists the **exercises**, the **variations & cues** (how to run it and what to watch for), and the **spring options**.

Because this is a mixed-level group, every block gives you a **base setting** plus how to **make it easier** (regress) and **harder** (advance) — so you can meet everyone in the same room without changing the choreography.

**Spring key:** 🔴 **R** = red (heavy) · 🔵 **B** = blue (medium) · 🟡 **Y** = yellow (light). Combinations read left to right, e.g. `RRR` = three reds, `RB` = red + blue.

> **Spring colours vary by brand.** This plan uses the R / B / Y convention from the Barre Body repertoire. If your studio uses a different colour system (e.g. Balanced Body green/blue/red/yellow), translate by **resistance level** — heavy / medium / light — not by colour name.

| 1 | Footwork foundations | Bilateral legs, heaviest load | ~8 min | 🔴🔴🔴R | up | — |
| 2 | Leg series — **right** | Unilateral legs, balance, glute | ~5 min | 🔴R | up | — |
| 3 | **Core** | Supine reset, feet on the bar | ~5 min | 🔴R | up | ball |

| 5 | Back + triceps | Pull work, postural counter | ~11 min | 🔵B | **down** | straps, box |
| — | Cooldown / stretch | Open hips, lengthen, breathe | ~5 min | 🔵B | down | box, ball |

### The circle

Sections 2 → 3 → 4 are the shape of this class. Hard standing work, lie down, hard standing work, then resolve.

```
        legs RIGHT (5)
              ↓
         CORE (5) ← supine reset, machine untouched
              ↓
        legs LEFT (5)
              ↓
          PIKE (2) ← both legs, closes the circle
```

**The whole circle runs on one machine setting.** Footbar up, 🔴R, for all 17 minutes. Nobody changes a spring, nobody moves the bar, nobody fetches a prop. The only thing that changes is which way they're facing.

Why split the legs at all:

- **Quality on side two.** Eleven continuous minutes of standing balance work degrades the stabilisers and the breath, not the muscle. Side two gets performed properly instead of survived.
- **It restores the supine moment.** Removing feet-in-straps took away the mid-class lie-down. This puts it back where it was, rather than tacking it on at minute 38.
- **Hard standing → lie down → hard standing is the biggest felt contrast in the class.** It's the bit people will remember.
- **The core block ends on hip extension**, which re-primes the glutes and opens the hip flexors right before they walk back into the lunges. The reset isn't just rest — it's the on-ramp.

### The other two arcs

**Three spring changes, all in the first 29 minutes.** 🔴🔴R → 🔴🔴🔴R → 🔴R → 🔵B. The footbar comes down at the same moment as the last one, so the two disruptions land together. **Nothing on the machine changes for the final 16 minutes.**

**Peak at two-thirds.** The fly lunge on the left side lands around minute 27 of 46. Everything after it steps down in intensity while still being real work — people leave accomplished rather than flattened.

## Warm-up / Mobility · ~5 min · 🔴🔴R · footbar up

**Purpose:** Connect breath and powerhouse, mobilise the spine, gently wake the legs. Plants the two motifs that recur all class — **the breath** and **the ball**.

**Exercises**

1. **Breathwork + Cat/Cow on the carriage** — lateral thoracic breathing, then big breath-led spinal articulation. No counting; move with the breath.
2. **Bridge + ball squeeze** — ball between the thighs; articulate the pelvis up and down, squeezing on the way up. Small heel lifts at the top to preview calf work. ~5–8 reps. **This returns as the last exercise of the core block.**
3. **Double leg press (easy tempo)** — parallel heels on the footbar, flowing tempo to build heat.

**Variations & cues**

- Keep Cat/Cow large and unhurried — it sets the breath rhythm for the whole class.
- Bridge: *"peel the spine off the carriage one vertebra at a time — a string of pearls."*
- Ball squeeze is a **connection cue**, not a max effort — *"gently hug the ball, feel your inner thighs answer."*

**Spring options**

| Level | Setting | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| Base | 🔴🔴R | One setting for the whole warm-up — nothing changes before class has started. |
| Easier | 🔴R | Lighter bridge load if the glutes are gripping. |

## Section 1 · Footwork Foundations · ~8 min · 🔴🔴🔴R · footbar up

**Purpose:** Bilateral, heavy leg work in the most stable position in the class. Load the legs most here, while everyone is fresh.

**Exercises**

1. **Double leg press series** — heels → toes/prehensile → **wide/turnout**.
2. **Heel raises** — slow eccentric lower, no bouncing.
3. **Prances** — roll one heel under as the opposite knee bends; rhythm and ankle mobility.
4. **Single leg press** — free leg at tabletop, pelvis level. Drop to 🔴🔴R.

> **Give the wide/turnout press real time — 8–10 slow reps, not a token few.** This is now the only dedicated adductor loading before the ball work later. It used to be covered by the frog press in feet-in-straps.

**Variations & cues**

- Press from *"the whole foot, not just the ball"* and control the return — *"resist the carriage home."*
- Heel raises: *"lift to the highest point over the big toe, lower with control below the bar line."*
- Turnout: *"rotate from the hip, not the knee — feel the inner thigh join in on the way back."*
- Single leg press is where the pelvis tips — *"both hip bones facing the ceiling, level like a tabletop."*

**Spring options**

| Level | Setting | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| Base (bilateral) | 🔴🔴🔴R | Heaviest block of the class — the stable position supports the load. |
| Single leg press | 🔴🔴R | Drop one red so one leg isn't carrying three. |
| Easier | 🔴🔴R throughout | Keeps knees comfortable; still plenty of work. |
| Advance | Hold 🔴🔴🔴R for single leg, slow the tempo | More load, longer time under tension. |

**Transition out (~30 seconds, not a section):** *"Come up to standing, spring down to one red, hands light on the bar."* You're already at 🔴🔴R after single leg press, so half the change has happened. **This setting now holds for 17 minutes.**

## Section 2 · Standing Leg Series — Right Side · ~5 min · 🔴R · footbar up

**Purpose:** Unilateral leg and glute work with a balance demand. Everything runs **hands on the footbar, facing the reformer** — one direction, no resets.

### Structure

> **long lunge → scooter → fly lunge → hold the long lunge and breathe**

Say the whole class shape out loud before you start:

*"Three moves on the right. Then we lie down for a bit. Then we match it on the left. Then one thing together."*

People brace when they don't know what's coming. Telling them the shape lets them commit — and knowing the lie-down is coming makes the right side easier to spend on.

### Setup

| Exercise | Base foot | Working foot | Intent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Long lunge** | Platform (front) | Carriage (back), slides long | **Range** — establish the shape |
| **Scooter** | Platform | Carriage, presses straight back, hips square | **Precision** — glute, no rotation |
| **Fly lunge** | Platform (front) | Carriage (back), long dynamic range | **Power** |

**Order matters.** Long lunge is the on-ramp — slow, grounded, teaches the shape. Scooter refines it: shorter range, hips locked square. Fly lunge loads it. Teach the shape, refine it, then power it.

**Dosage:** ~60–75 seconds each, then a 20-second hold.

### The stretch is built into the work

After the fly lunge, **don't come out of it.** Sink into the long lunge and hold for 20 seconds, breathing. That's the hip flexor and hamstring you just worked, released in the position you're already standing in. Work → work → peak → release, without moving.

### Variations & cues

- **Long lunge vs fly lunge:** same shape, different intent. Long lunge is controlled and grounded. Fly lunge is dynamic and light through the back leg.
- **Scooter:** *"square the hips forward like headlights, glute of the standing leg switched on."* Shorter range than the lunges, on purpose.
- **Control the carriage home in every one.** *"Push out with power, come home with control."* No slamming.
- *"The footbar is a balance point, not a handle — fingertips, not fists."* Unless someone needs it as an anchor, in which case let them grip and mean it.

**Spring options**

| Level | Setting | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| Base | 🔴R | Set at the end of footwork. Holds for the whole circle. |
| Easier / balance-limited | 🔴🔴R + hand firmly on the bar | Heavier pins the carriage; the bar is a real anchor. |
| Advance | 🔵B | Lighter carriage = more range and much more stability demand. |

> **Choose once, keep it for 17 minutes.** Say so: *"whatever you pick now stays with you through the lunges, the core, and the other side."*

**Transition out:** step down, lie back on the carriage, pick up the ball. **Nothing on the machine moves.**

## Section 3 · Core · ~5 min · 🔴R · footbar up · ball

**Purpose:** The supine reset in the middle of the circle. Real work, but it feels like relief — and it counters what the legs just did rather than adding to it.

**The footbar is still up, so use it.** Feet on the bar turns the carriage into the feedback mechanism: press unevenly and it moves. *"The carriage doesn't move"* is a better core cue than anything you can say about navels, because it's self-correcting and they can feel it instantly.

**Exercises**

1. **Hundred** — heels on the footbar (legs long) or tabletop, ball between the thighs, arms pumping.
2. **Chest lift + oblique reach** — feet on the footbar, ball in the hands, reach past the opposite knee. *"The carriage doesn't move."*
3. **Roll back and hover** — sit facing the footbar, **feet pressing into the bar**, ball in the hands as the reach target. Roll back to a hover, then roll up.
4. **Bridge on the footbar + ball squeeze** — ball to the thighs. Single-leg to advance.

**Flexion → rotation → articulation → extension.** The block ends on hip extension, which is exactly what fires the glutes and opens the hip flexors before the second leg side. **The reset isn't just rest — it's the on-ramp.**

**Variations & cues**

- **Roll back is the useful half of a teaser without the exposure.** The eccentric is where the work is, everyone can succeed at it, and strong clients add a full roll-up on their own without being told.
- **Press the heels into the bar as you roll up** — *"let the hamstrings help, not the hip flexors."* This is the whole point: hamstring engagement reciprocally quiets the hip flexors, which the lunges need back. It's why roll-back belongs here and teaser doesn't.
- **The ball between the ankles or thighs is doing real work** — squeezing fires the adductors, which is where this class recovers the inner-thigh work that left with the frog press.
- Bridge: *"we started class here — this is where it comes back."*

**Spring options**

| Level | Setting | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| Base | 🔴R | Unchanged from Section 2. Red holds the carriage firmly home — ideal for feet-on-bar work. |
| Advance | Single-leg bridge, longer levers in the hundred | Load without touching the springs. |
| Easier | Tabletop instead of long legs; roll back only halfway | Shorten the lever before reducing the reps. |

**Transition out:** *"Ball down, back up to standing, other side."* **Nothing on the machine moves.**

## Section 4 · Standing Leg Series — Left Side + Pike · ~7 min · 🔴R · footbar up

### Left side (~5 min)

**Same three, same order, same spring:** long lunge → scooter → fly lunge → hold and breathe.

**What to cue differently on side two:**

- **Talk less.** They know the shapes now. Cue quality and breath, not setup. Silence is allowed.
- **Watch for the "catch-up" instinct** — people rush side two to finish. *"Same tempo as the right. We're not in a hurry."*
- **Expect side two to feel different**, and say so before anyone worries about it. *"One side always feels stronger. That's information, not a problem."*
- This is where the class peaks. Name it at the fly lunge: *"this is the hardest thing we do today."* People push harder and leave prouder.

### Pike — the close (~2 min)

Both feet come onto the carriage. Hands stay on the footbar. Same spring.

**Pike ladder** — everyone starts at rung one and climbs as far as they own it:

| Rung | Version |
|---|---|
| 1 | Plank hold, both feet on the carriage |
| 2 | Single knee tuck, alternating |
| 3 | Full pike, hips high |

**Hold the last one for 20 seconds and breathe.** Pike is already a hamstring and calf lengthener — the reward for the leg work is the leg work.

**Why pike is here and not in the arm block:**

- **It's bilateral.** After twelve minutes of one-side-at-a-time, a symmetrical move resolves the body. It closes the circle.
- **It's the hinge.** They finish it kneeling-ready, facing the footbar — exactly where the arm block starts. They don't turn around.

**Watch-point:** wrists and anterior shoulder. Offer forearms on the footbar, or a standing calf raise, for anyone who needs out.

**Transition out:** *"Footbar down, springs to blue, straps in your hands — take a breath, we're changing gears."* One breath cycle. **This is the last change of the class.**

## Section 5 · Back + Triceps · ~11 min · 🔵B · footbar **down** · straps + box

**Purpose:** The postural counter. The pike just loaded anterior shoulders, triceps and wrists — so this block pulls. Back is the backbone of the section, triceps rides along in the same position, biceps is a short finisher.

### Position A — kneeling, facing the footbar (~5 min)

They stay exactly where the pike left them. Straps come from behind. No box yet.

1. **Arms pull back** — straight arms, draw down and back. Lats.
2. **Chest expansion** — arms by the sides, press back and hold; option to turn the head side to side on the hold. Feels enormous after thirty minutes of facing forward and pressing.
3. **Tricep press back** — same arm line, just add the elbow bend. **Zero transition** from chest expansion.

### Position B — seated on the short box, facing the risers (~6 min)

Box out, one turn, sit down. Sitting after kneeling reads as a break even though the work continues, and the box gives a taller, better-supported spine for the pulls than sitting on the carriage does.

4. **Seated row** — rhomboids and mid back.
5. **Reverse fly** — arms wide, rear delts.
6. **Biceps curl** — 90 seconds, the dessert. Ends the working part of the class on a pump.

**Variations & cues**

- *"Melt the shoulder blades down into your back pockets before you pull."* Say it once per exercise — scapula creep is the failure mode in every move here.
- Chest expansion: *"lengthen the collarbones wide, breathe into the front of the chest."*
- Tricep press back: *"elbows stay pinned where they are — only the forearm moves."*
- Control the eccentric everywhere. *"The straps want to pull you back. Don't let them win."*
- **Two positions for six exercises.** Don't add a third; the flow is the point.
- **The box stays out** — the mermaid uses it.

**Spring options**

| Level | Setting | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| Base | 🔵B | One setting for all six exercises. |
| Easier | 🟡Y, or shorten the range | Lighter load, especially for reverse fly and curls. |
| Advance | Slow the tempo, pause at end range | Cheaper than a spring change this late in class. |

> **Optional for strong groups only:** a full **teaser** off the box or carriage at the very end of this block. The legs are done by now, so hip flexor fatigue no longer costs you anything. Cut it freely — the class does not need it.

## Cooldown / Stretch · ~5 min · 🔵B · footbar down

**Purpose:** Open the hip flexors the lunge series hammered, lengthen the side body, settle the breath. **Descend through the positions** — seated, then kneeling, then supine. End horizontal.

**Exercises**

1. **Mermaid** — seated on the box, which is already out. Side-body opening, both sides.
2. **Kneeling hip flexor / quad stretch** — box away. Back knee on the carriage, front foot on the platform, hands on the footbar posts; sink forward and let the carriage travel a little. **Non-negotiable after the lunge series** — the back leg's hip flexor worked hard on both sides. (Floor version is fine if the group is unsteady.)
3. **Supine figure-4** — lie back on the carriage.
4. **Supine spinal twist** — knees to one side, breathe.
5. **Final breaths** — ball resting on the belly, feel it rise and fall. Closes both motifs: the breathwork that opened class, and the ball.

**Variations & cues**

- *"Breathe into the stretch — exhale to soften a little deeper each time."*
- Hip flexor: *"tuck the tail underneath you first, then reach the same-side arm up and over."* Without the tuck they arch instead of stretching.
- Mermaid: *"reach up and over, feel the ribs open on the stretched side."*
- **End on length, not on burn.** The last thing they feel is the thing they carry out the door.

**Spring options**

| Setting | Why |
|---------|-----|
| 🔵B | Unchanged since minute 29. The spring is support here, not resistance. |

## Teaching Notes

### Spring & footbar flow

**🔴🔴R → 🔴🔴🔴R → 🔴🔴R → 🔴R → 🔵B.** Three real changes, all in the first 29 minutes. The footbar comes down once, at the same moment as the last spring change, so the two disruptions land together instead of separately. From the top of Section 5 to the final breath — 16 minutes — nothing on the machine changes.

The circle (Sections 2–4, 17 minutes) runs entirely untouched.

### Thirteen levers for making the class *feel* good

These are the choices baked into the plan. Pull them deliberately.

1. **Split the hardest block in half.** The circle is the single biggest change in this version. Hard → lie down → hard beats eleven minutes of grinding.
2. **Peak at two-thirds, not the end.** Fly lunge on the left, ~minute 27 of 46.
3. **Build the stretch into the work.** Hold the long lunge after the fly lunge; hold the last pike. The reward for the block is the block.
4. **Make the rest block point where you're going.** The core section ends on hip extension, which re-primes the glutes for the lunges.
5. **Position change reads as rest.** Supine → standing → supine → standing → kneeling → seated → supine. Seven changes of relationship to gravity.
6. **Tell them the whole shape up front.** *"Three moves right, lie down, match it left, one thing together."* Knowing the lie-down is coming makes side one easier to spend on.
7. **Name the peak out loud.** *"This is the hardest thing we do today."*
8. **Talk less on side two.** They know it. Cue quality and breath, not setup.
9. **Tempo variety beats rep count.** Slow eccentric → pulses → hold.
10. **One spring per block.** Three changes in 46 minutes; none in the last 16.
11. **Recurring motifs.** The bridge (warm-up, core block). The ball (warm-up, core, final breath). The breath (opening, closing). People feel structure even when they can't name it.
12. **Bilateral closure after unilateral.** The pike resolves twelve minutes of one-side-at-a-time.
13. **End on length, not burn**, lying down.

### Mixed-level strategy

Choreography stays the same for everyone — you differentiate with **spring** and **range/tempo**, not different exercises. Set expectations at the top: *"the movement is the same for all of us today; your spring and your range are yours to choose."*

The three ladders — **single leg press**, **pike**, **roll back and hover** — are where the room spreads out. Everyone starts at rung one and climbs as far as they own it, so nobody is visibly doing a "beginner exercise." That's deliberate: this class has no move where a person can publicly fail.

### Watch-points

- **Pelvis level** in single-leg press and every standing move.
- **Hips square** in scooter — it wants to rotate open.
- **Control the carriage home** in all lunge and press work. No slamming.
- **Hip flexors** through the whole leg series — if the low back arches in a lunge, shorten the range rather than pushing through.
- **Carriage still** in the core block. If it drifts, the pressure through the feet is uneven.
- **Scapula depressed** — shoulders out of ears — in every strap exercise and in the pike.
- **Knee over second toe** in every lunge.

### Modifications & contraindications

- **Pregnancy (past first trimester):** no prolonged supine and no loaded spinal flexion. Swap the core block for side-lying leg work or seated ball work; incline/wedge for the warm-up bridge; skip the pike. The standing leg series is fine with a wider base and a hand on the bar.
- **Lower-back sensitivity:** halve the roll-back range or replace it with a supine march. Shorten range in the lunges before adding spring. Imprint in supine work.
- **Knee sensitivity:** partial range in footwork and lunges; keep the knee tracking over the toes. Pad under the knee in the kneeling strap block and the hip flexor stretch.
- **Wrist/shoulder sensitivity:** the pike is the pressure point — offer forearms on the footbar, or a standing calf raise instead. Keep loads light in rows and curls.
- **Balance / newer clients:** heavier spring in the standing series (more supportive), hand firmly on the bar, and permission to keep the back foot lighter in the lunges.

## What changed from v2, and why

| Change | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Squat / sumo section cut** | The escalator logic was wrong. In a carriage squat **both feet are on the moving surface**; in a lunge or scooter the standing foot is on the stationary platform. The squat was the *less* stable option, not the gentler step. Footwork already covers bilateral quad and turnout load. |
| **Long lunge is now the on-ramp** | With squats gone, the first standing move needs to teach the shape. Long lunge does — slow and grounded. Then scooter refines it, fly lunge loads it. |
| **Leg series split around the core block** | The circle. Quality on side two, restores the mid-class supine moment lost with the straps, and gives the class its biggest felt contrast. |
| **Core moved to the middle instead of arms** | Arms in the middle would cost four machine changes (footbar up→down→up, spring R→B→R→B) plus double box handling. Core costs **zero** — same footbar, same spring, prop already on the floor. |
| **Core now uses the footbar** | With the bar still up, feet-on-bar makes the carriage the feedback mechanism. *"The carriage doesn't move"* beats any verbal core cue. |
| **Teaser cut, replaced with roll back and hover** | Teaser is hip-flexor dominant and would pre-fatigue the second leg side. It's also a pass/fail move in a class built around feeling good, and the strap-free version doesn't use the machine. Roll-back keeps the eccentric — the useful half — and **pressing the heels into the bar recruits hamstrings, which quiets the hip flexors** instead of loading them. |
| **Box appears once, at the end** | Out for the seated arm work, stays for the mermaid, away before the supine cooldown. One setup, one put-away. |
| **Arm block grew to 11 min** | Absorbed the time freed by cutting squats. |

### What the strap removal costs, and where it's covered

| Lost | Covered by |
|---|---|
| Long-lever hamstring load | Scooter, the pike hold, and heel-press in the roll-back |
| Adductors (frog press) | Wide/turnout footwork, given real time, + the ball in the core block |
| The supine mid-class recovery | **Fully restored** — the core block sits exactly where feet-in-straps used to |
| Spinal articulation (short spine) | Bridge in the warm-up, roll back and hover, bridge again in the core block |
