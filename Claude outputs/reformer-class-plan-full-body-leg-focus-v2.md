# Reformer Class Plan — Full Body, Leg Focus (v2 · Standing Flow Redesign)

**Duration:** ~46 min · **Level:** Mixed / all levels · **Style:** Group reformer

**Props:** Straps (hands only) · Small ball

**Setup note:** Footbar stays **up** from the first breath through the pike, then comes **down once** for the rest of the class. The small ball is needed in the warm-up and again in the core block — keep it on the floor by the shoulder blocks all class. No box.

> **This is v2.** Feet in straps has been removed. The leg work is now a single standing series (long lunge → scooter → fly lunge → pike up) and the upper body/core block has been expanded. See *What changed and why* at the end.

---

## How to read this plan

Each section lists the **exercises**, the **variations & cues** (how to run it and what to watch for), and the **spring options**.

Because this is a mixed-level group, every block gives you a **base setting** plus how to **make it easier** (regress) and **harder** (advance) — so you can meet everyone in the same room without changing the choreography.

**Spring key:** 🔴 **R** = red (heavy) · 🔵 **B** = blue (medium) · 🟡 **Y** = yellow (light). Combinations read left to right, e.g. `RRR` = three reds, `RB` = red + blue.

> **Spring colours vary by brand.** This plan uses the R / B / Y convention from the Barre Body repertoire. If your studio uses a different colour system (e.g. Balanced Body green/blue/red/yellow), translate by **resistance level** — heavy / medium / light — not by colour name.

## Class at a glance

| # | Section | Focus | Time | Spring | Footbar |
|---|---------|-------|------|--------|---------|
| — | Warm-up / mobility | Breath, spine, wake up the legs | ~5 min | 🔴🔴R | up |
| 1 | Footwork foundations | Bilateral legs, heaviest load | ~8 min | 🔴🔴🔴R | up |
| 2 | Standing bilateral | Get upright, squats & sumo | ~3 min | 🔴R | up |
| 3 | Standing lunge series | Unilateral legs → bilateral pike | ~11 min | 🔴R | up |
| 4 | Back + triceps | Pull work, postural counter | ~10 min | 🔵B | **down** |
| 5 | Core (ball) | Strap-free, teaser peak | ~5 min | 🔵B | down |
| — | Cooldown / stretch | Open hips, lengthen, breathe | ~4 min | 🔵B | down |

### The three arcs that make this class work

**1 · The leg escalator.** Supine bilateral (footwork) → standing bilateral (squats) → standing unilateral (lunge series) → back to bilateral (pike). Load goes heavy to light while stability demand goes low to high. Nobody is asked to balance on a moving carriage until their legs are warm and they've already stood on it.

**2 · Three spring changes, total.** 🔴🔴R → 🔴🔴🔴R → 🔴R → 🔵B, and that last change happens at ~minute 27. **Nothing changes for the final 19 minutes of class.** One footbar change, at the same moment.

**3 · Peak at two-thirds, then descend.** The fly lunge is the hardest thing in the class and it lands around minute 25 of 46 — not at the end. Everything after it steps down in intensity while still being real work, so people leave accomplished rather than flattened.

## Warm-up / Mobility · ~5 min · 🔴🔴R · footbar up

**Purpose:** Connect breath and powerhouse, mobilise the spine, gently wake the legs. Also plants the ball — it comes back at the very end of class as the bookend.

**Exercises**

1. **Breathwork + Cat/Cow on the carriage** — lateral thoracic breathing, then big breath-led spinal articulation. No counting — move with the breath.
2. **Bridge + ball squeeze** — ball between the thighs; articulate the pelvis up and down, squeezing on the way up to switch on glutes and inner thighs. Add small heel lifts at the top to preview calf work. ~5–8 reps. **Remember this one — it returns as the last exercise of the core block.**
3. **Double leg press (easy tempo)** — parallel heels on the footbar, flowing tempo to build heat and hint at what's coming.

**Variations & cues**

- Keep Cat/Cow large and unhurried — this sets the breath rhythm for the whole class.
- Bridge: *"peel the spine off the carriage one vertebra at a time — a string of pearls."*
- Ball squeeze is a **connection cue**, not a max effort — *"gently hug the ball, feel your inner thighs answer."*

**Spring options**

| Level | Setting | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| Base | 🔴🔴R | One setting for the whole warm-up — no changes before the class has started. |
| Easier | 🔴R | Lighter bridge load if the glutes are gripping. |

## Section 1 · Footwork Foundations · ~8 min · 🔴🔴🔴R · footbar up

**Purpose:** Bilateral, heavy leg work in the most stable position in the class — so we load the legs most here, while everyone is fresh.

**Exercises**

1. **Double leg press series** — heels → toes/prehensile → **wide/turnout**.
2. **Heel raises** — slow eccentric lower, no bouncing.
3. **Prances** — roll one heel under as the opposite knee bends; rhythm and ankle mobility.
4. **Single leg press** — free leg at tabletop; keep the pelvis level. Drop to 🔴🔴R.

> **Give the wide/turnout press real time — 8–10 slow reps, not a token few.** This is now the only dedicated adductor loading before the ball work at the very end. It used to be covered by the frog press in feet-in-straps.

**Variations & cues**

- Press from *"the whole foot, not just the ball"* and control the return — *"resist the carriage home."*
- Heel raises: *"lift to the highest point over the big toe, lower with control below the bar line."*
- Turnout: *"rotate from the hip, not the knee — feel the inner thigh join in on the way back."*
- Single leg press is where the pelvis tips — *"both hip bones facing the ceiling, level like a tabletop."*

**Spring options**

| Level | Setting | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| Base (bilateral) | 🔴🔴🔴R | Heaviest block of the class — the stable position supports the load. |
| Single leg press | 🔴🔴R | Drop one red so one leg isn't carrying three. |
| Easier | 🔴🔴R throughout | Keeps knees comfortable; still plenty of work. |
| Advance | Hold 🔴🔴🔴R for single leg, slow the tempo | More load, longer time under tension. |

**Transition out:** you're already at 🔴🔴R after single leg press — drop to 🔴R and stand up. Half the spring change has already happened.

## Section 2 · Standing Bilateral · ~3 min · 🔴R · footbar up

**Purpose:** The escalator step. **Do not skip this to save time.** It gets people upright and standing on the carriage while both feet are still down, before you ask anyone to balance on one leg on a moving surface. It also sets the spring they will now live on for the next 14 minutes.

**Exercises**

1. **Squats (parallel)** — standing on the carriage facing the footbar, feet hip-width, heels near the shoulder blocks.
2. **Sumo squat** — feet wide, outside the blocks, toes turned out ~45°.

**Variations & cues**

- *"The footbar is a balance point, not a handle — fingertips, not fists."*
- Parallel drives the quads; turnout shifts load into adductors and glutes. Knees track over the second toe in both.
- Call the spring choice here and be explicit that it stays: *"whatever you pick now, you keep for the whole standing series — choose something you can push against but still move."*

**Spring options**

| Level | Setting | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| Base | 🔴R | Enough to push against, light enough to travel. |
| Easier / less confident | 🔴🔴R | Heavier pins the carriage — more stable underfoot. |
| Advance | 🔵B | Lighter = carriage travels = **more** stability demand. |

## Section 3 · Standing Lunge Series + Pike · ~11 min · 🔴R · footbar up

**Purpose:** The heart of the class. Unilateral leg and glute work with a balance demand, finishing with a bilateral pike that both evens the body out and hands you into the next block.

**Orientation: hands on the footbar, facing the reformer, for all four exercises.** One direction, one spring, one footbar position, no resets.

### Structure

> **Right side:** long lunge → scooter → fly lunge → hold the long lunge and breathe
> **Left side:** the same three → hold and breathe
> **Both:** pike up

Say this out loud before you start. *"Three moves on the right, then we match it on the left, then one thing together."* People brace when they don't know the shape — telling them lets them commit.

### Setup

| Exercise | Base foot | Working foot | Intent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Long lunge** | Platform (front) | Carriage (back), slides long | **Range** — establish the shape |
| **Scooter** | Platform | Carriage, presses straight back, hips square | **Precision** — glute, no rotation |
| **Fly lunge** | Platform (front) | Carriage (back), long dynamic range | **Power** — the peak of the class |
| **Pike up** | Both feet on the carriage | — | **Closure** — bilateral, lengthens, hinges out |

**Dosage:** ~45–60 seconds each. A genuine **15-second reset between sides** — cued breath, not dead air. It buys you their attention for side two.

### The two moments that make this section feel good

**1 · The stretch is built into the work.** After the fly lunge, don't switch straight away — sink into the long lunge and hold it for 20 seconds. That's the hip flexor and hamstring you just worked, released in the position you're already in. Work → work → peak → release, twice.

**2 · Hold the last pike.** Pike is already a hamstring and calf lengthener. Hold the final one for 20 seconds and breathe. The reward for the leg block *is* the leg block.

### Variations & cues

- **Long lunge vs fly lunge:** same shape, different intent. Long lunge is controlled and grounded — find the range. Fly lunge is dynamic and light through the back leg — this is where you name it: *"this is the hardest thing we do today."*
- **Scooter:** *"square the hips forward like headlights, glute of the standing leg switched on."* Shorter range than the lunges, on purpose.
- **Control the carriage home in every single one.** No slamming. *"Push out with power, come home with control."*
- **Pike ladder** — everyone starts at the bottom rung and climbs as far as they own it:
  1. Plank hold, both feet on the carriage
  2. Single knee tuck, alternating
  3. Full pike, hips high

**Spring options**

| Level | Setting | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| Base | 🔴R | Carried straight over from squats — **no change entering this block.** |
| Easier / balance-limited | 🔴🔴R + hand firmly on the footbar | Heavier pins the carriage; the bar is a real anchor. |
| Advance | 🔵B | Lighter carriage = more range and much more stability demand. |

> **One spring for the entire block, both sides, including the pike.** Set it in Section 2 and leave it. This section is about flow and balance, not spring maths.

**Transition out:** after the pike, they are already kneeling-ready and facing the footbar. Footbar down, springs to 🔵B, pick up the straps — **they don't turn around.**

## Section 4 · Back + Triceps · ~10 min · 🔵B · footbar **down**

**Purpose:** The postural counter. The pike just loaded anterior shoulders, triceps and wrists — so this block pulls. Back is the backbone of the section, triceps rides along in the same position, biceps is a short finisher.

**Take one full breath cycle while the footbar lowers.** Say it: *"footbar down, springs to blue, straps in your hands — take a breath, we're changing gears."* This is the only spring change left in the class.

### Position A — kneeling, facing the footbar (~5 min)

They stay exactly where the pike left them. Straps come from behind.

1. **Arms pull back** — straight arms, draw down and back. Lats.
2. **Chest expansion** — arms by the sides, press back and hold; option to turn the head side to side on the hold. Feels enormous after 25 minutes of facing forward and pressing.
3. **Tricep press back** — same arm line, just add the elbow bend. **Zero transition** from chest expansion.

### Position B — seated, facing the risers (~5 min)

One turn. Sitting down after kneeling reads as a break even though the work continues.

4. **Seated row** — rhomboids and mid back.
5. **Reverse fly** — arms wide, rear delts.
6. **Biceps curl** — 90 seconds, the dessert. Ends the working part of the class on a pump.

**Variations & cues**

- *"Melt the shoulder blades down into your back pockets before you pull."* Say it once per exercise — scapula creep is the failure mode in every move here.
- Chest expansion: *"lengthen the collarbones wide, breathe into the front of the chest."*
- Tricep press back: *"elbows stay pinned where they are — only the forearm moves."*
- Control the eccentric everywhere. *"The straps want to pull you back — don't let them win."*
- **Two orientations for six exercises.** Don't add a third; the flow is the point.

**Spring options**

| Level | Setting | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| Base | 🔵B | One setting for all six exercises. |
| Easier | 🟡Y or shorten the range | Lighter load, especially for reverse fly and curls. |
| Advance | 🔴R for row and arms pull back only | Only if you're willing to spend the spring change — otherwise slow the tempo instead. |

## Section 5 · Core · ~5 min · 🔵B · footbar down · **ball**

**Purpose:** Strap-free, bodyweight core on the carriage. After 35 minutes of spring-loaded everything, this is a genuine change of texture — it feels calm rather than like one more loaded exercise. Building to teaser gives people a signature move to have done.

**No spring change, no setup.** Put the straps down, pick up the ball, lie back.

**Exercises — one ball, three jobs**

1. **Hundred** — ball between the ankles, legs at the level they own (tabletop → 45° → long).
2. **Oblique reach / twist** — ball in the hands, reach past the opposite knee.
3. **Teaser ladder** — ball back between the ankles.
4. **Bridge + ball squeeze** — ball to the thighs. **The warm-up bookend.**

**Teaser ladder** — same shape for everyone, three rungs:

| Rung | Version |
|---|---|
| 1 | Roll-back only, feet down, hands reaching along the thighs |
| 2 | Single-leg teaser |
| 3 | Full teaser, ball squeezed between the ankles |

**Variations & cues**

- **The ball between the ankles is doing real work, not just occupying the feet.** Squeezing it fires the adductors, and adductor connection is what makes a roll-up go instead of stall. It's also where the class recovers the inner-thigh work that left with the frog press.
- **Cue the roll-down, not the roll-up:** *"lower one vertebra at a time — the way down is the work."* Mixed-level clients can succeed at the eccentric even when the roll-up needs momentum. Nobody has to fail publicly.
- *"Don't push into the shoulder blocks"* — on 🔵B the carriage sits home on its own as long as nobody braces backwards into the blocks.
- The bridge is the release from all that flexion as well as the bookend. Call it: *"we started here — let's finish here."*

**Spring options**

| Level | Setting | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| Base | 🔵B | Carried over from Section 4 — **no change.** Carriage sits anchored under a still body. |
| If your studio's medium is very light | Add a spring so the carriage stays home | Stability, not resistance. |

---

## Cooldown / Stretch · ~4 min · 🔵B · footbar down

**Purpose:** Open the hip flexors the lunge series hammered, lengthen the side body, settle the breath. **Stay lying down as long as possible** — horizontal endings feel restorative.

**Exercises**

1. **Supine figure-4** — straight on from the bridge, no position change at all.
2. **Supine spinal twist** — knees to one side, breathe.
3. **Kneeling hip flexor / quad stretch** — non-negotiable after the lunge series; the back leg's hip flexor worked hard on both sides.
4. **Mermaid** — kneeling on the carriage, side-body opening.
5. **Three slow breaths** — seated, or invite them to lie back down.

**Variations & cues**

- *"Breathe into the stretch — exhale to soften a little deeper each time."*
- Hip flexor: *"tuck the tail underneath you first, then reach the same-side arm up and over."* Without the tuck they arch instead of stretching.
- Mermaid: *"reach up and over, feel the ribs open on the stretched side."*
- **End on length, not on burn.** The last thing they feel is the thing they remember.

**Spring options**

| Setting | Why |
|---------|-----|
| 🔵B | Unchanged since minute 27. The spring is support here, not resistance. |

## Teaching Notes

### Spring & footbar flow

**🔴🔴R → 🔴🔴🔴R → 🔴🔴R → 🔴R → 🔵B.** Three real changes, all in the first 27 minutes. The footbar comes down once, at the same moment as the last spring change, so the two disruptions land together instead of separately. From the top of Section 4 to the final breath — 19 minutes — nothing on the machine changes.

### Twelve levers for making the class *feel* good

These are the choices baked into the plan. Pull them deliberately.

1. **Peak at two-thirds, not the end.** Fly lunge at ~minute 25 of 46.
2. **Build the stretch into the work.** Hold the long lunge after fly lunge; hold the last pike. The reward for the block is the block.
3. **Position change reads as rest.** Supine → standing → kneeling → seated → supine. Five positions, each one feels like a breath.
4. **Tell them the shape.** *"Three on the right, match it on the left, then one together."* Uncertainty is what makes people brace.
5. **Name the peak out loud.** *"This is the hardest thing we do today."* People push harder and leave prouder.
6. **Tempo variety beats rep count.** Slow eccentric → pulses → hold. Same fatigue, far better feeling than 20 reps at one speed.
7. **One spring per block.** Three changes in 46 minutes.
8. **Bookend it.** Bridge + ball squeeze opens and closes. People notice.
9. **Bilateral closure after unilateral.** The pike evens out eight minutes of one-side-at-a-time. It resolves.
10. **Finish horizontal.** Core and the first half of cooldown are on their backs.
11. **A real 15 seconds between sides.** Cued breath, not dead air.
12. **End on length, not burn.**

### Mixed-level strategy

Choreography stays the same for everyone — you differentiate with **spring** and **range/tempo**, not different exercises. Set expectations at the top: *"the movement is the same for all of us today; your spring and your range are yours to choose."*

The three ladders (pike, teaser, single-leg press) are where the room spreads out. Everyone starts at rung one and climbs as far as they own it — nobody is doing a "beginner exercise."

### Watch-points

- **Pelvis level** in single-leg press and every standing move.
- **Hips square** in scooter — it wants to rotate open.
- **Control the carriage home** in all lunge and press work. No slamming.
- **Scapula depressed** — shoulders out of ears — in every strap exercise and in plank/pike.
- **Knee over second toe** in squats, sumo and lunges.
- **Nobody bracing into the shoulder blocks** during the core block.

### Modifications & contraindications

- **Pregnancy (past first trimester):** no teaser and no prolonged supine. Swap the core block for side-lying leg work or seated ball work; use an incline/wedge in the warm-up bridge; skip the pike. No deep spinal flexion under load.
- **Lower-back sensitivity:** teaser rung 1 (roll-back only) or a supine march instead. Shorten range in the lunges before adding spring.
- **Knee sensitivity:** partial range in footwork, squats and lunges; keep the knee tracking over the toes. Offer a pad under the knee in the kneeling strap block.
- **Wrist/shoulder sensitivity:** the pike is the pressure point — offer plank on forearms off the footbar, or skip to a standing calf raise. Keep loads light in rows and curls.
- **Balance / newer clients:** heavier spring in the standing series (more supportive), hand firmly on the footbar, and permission to keep the back foot lighter in the lunges.

## What changed from v1, and why

| Change | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Feet in straps removed** | Client request. Frees ~8 min for the standing series and the expanded upper body block. |
| **Squats/sumo split into their own section** | v1 buried them inside the standing block. They're now the deliberate escalator step between supine and unilateral work — and they set the spring for the next 14 minutes. |
| **Lateral lunge and side splits cut** | They required a 90° turn mid-block, which was the awkward moment in v1. Everything now faces one direction. |
| **New leg flow: long lunge → scooter → fly lunge → pike** | Client request. Range → precision → power → length, all with hands on the footbar. |
| **Pike moved out of Section 4 into the leg block** | It's the bilateral closer *and* the hinge into the arm work — they finish the pike already facing the right way to kneel and pick up the straps. |
| **Upper body expanded to 6 exercises, back-led** | Pike loads anterior shoulders and triceps, so the block that follows should pull. Two orientations only. |
| **Core is strap-free and ball-led, teaser as the peak** | Client request — no straps. Bodyweight core is a change of texture after 35 min of loaded work. |
| **Spring changes cut from 4 to 3, all in the first 27 min** | Nothing on the machine changes for the last 19 minutes. |

### What the strap removal costs, and where it's covered

| Lost | Covered by |
|---|---|
| Long-lever hamstring load | Scooter and the pike hold |
| Adductors (frog press) | Wide/turnout footwork (given real time) + ball between the ankles in the core block |
| The supine mid-class recovery | Moved, not lost — the core block is now the lie-down moment, landing late instead of never |
| Spinal articulation (short spine) | Bridge in the warm-up and again as the core bookend |
