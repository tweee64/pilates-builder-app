# Reformer Class Plan — Teacher Training Assessment (45 min)

Discipline: Reformer · Length: 45 minutes
Built from the instructor's own exercise selection, sequenced and timed against
[assessment-criteria.md](../assessment-criteria.md) and the sequencing/spring rules in
[AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) §5.2–5.3. All cues, timings, scripts, music and layering choices
below are written in-house. No prose is taken from the Reformer manual.

**Structural decision:** the lower-body block is split in half, with the core/plank block between
the two sides (Section 2a → Section 3 → Section 2b). This is deliberate — see §2.

---

## 1. Pre-class: introduction & screening — 2 min

Keep this tight. At 45 minutes you cannot afford a three-minute welcome, but you also cannot skip
the screening — it's a scored item and it's the safety basis for everything that follows.

> "Hi everyone, I'm [name] — welcome in. Quick check before we start: anyone brand new to
> Reformer? Anyone pregnant, or working around an injury — back, shoulder, wrist, knee? Hand up or
> catch my eye and I'll tell you exactly what to set and give you an option. You're changing your
> own springs today — there's only one change, I'll call it out loud and show you on mine, so
> nothing to work out on your own. Grab a small ball for your reformer too — we'll use it all class. We've got
> kneeling and standing on the carriage today, so if knees are sensitive grab a pad now."

*(Pilates Principle — Breath/Centering: gives students a moment to arrive before the first cue.)*

**Screening flags to carry through the class:**

| Flag | Where it matters | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Pregnancy | §4 Core (supine), §5/§7 Legs, §6 plank block | Shorten supine work, wedge under the shoulders, reduce spring, substitute bridges with a side-lying option |
| Wrist | §6 entire plank block, Elephant, Down Stretch, §8 Mountain Climber (optional) | Fists instead of flat palms, or forearms on the plank/push-up |
| Shoulder | §4 straps block, §8 Back block (both on one red), Long Stretch, push-up | Sit closer to the straps in §8, keep the High Row low, skip the optional Reverse Fly, small press (or none) on the Press Up, push-up from knees |
| Knee | Scooter, Down Stretch, kneeling hip flexor stretch, ball squeeze | Knee pad, shorter range, carriage closer to home; ball between the thighs rather than at the knee |
| Low back | Bridges and bridge press-out, Section 1 leg extensions & scissors, Hundred, Down Stretch | Smaller bridge range and a small press-out, stay in the over-the-bar set only, legs higher in the Hundred, less extension in Down Stretch |

## 2. Class architecture — running clock

| Clock | Segment | Time | Spring | Footbar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Intro & screening | 2 min | — | — |
| 2:00 | Warm-up — footwork & bridges | 5.5 min | **RR** | Up |
| 7:30 | Section 1 — Core, hands in straps | 7 min | **R** *(the only change)* | Up *(carried)* |
| 14:30 | Section 2a — Legs, first side | 5.5 min | R *(carried)* | Up |
| 20:00 | Section 3 — Core, plank family | 6 min | R *(carried)* | Up |
| 26:00 | Section 2b — Legs, second side | 5.5 min | R *(carried)* | Up |
| 31:30 | Section 4 — Back chain & arms | 8 min | R *(carried)* | Down |
| 39:30 | Cooldown | 4.5 min | R *(carried)* | Down |
| 44:00 | Outro | 1 min | — | — |
| **45:00** | **End** | **45 min exactly** | **1 spring change** | 1 bar change |

**The two checkpoints to memorise:** you should be **starting the second leg side at 26:00**, and
**starting the cooldown at 39:30**. If you're behind at either, drop Section 4's optional
exercises first, then cut reps there — not in Section 2b, and not in the cooldown.

**Why split the legs.** Three reasons, worth saying out loud if the assessor asks:

1. **Recovery for the shoulder girdle.** Down Stretch, Long Stretch, push-up and the two Elephants
   are five consecutive wrist- and shoulder-loaded exercises, and Section 4 is eight minutes of
   pulling. The second leg side gives the upper body a real break before it has to row.
2. **Zero transition cost.** Every junction matches the position either side of it: Flying Lunge
   (hands on bar) → Down Stretch (kneeling, hands on bar), then One-Leg Elephant
   (standing, hands on bar) → Long Lunge (standing). Nothing is jolted.
3. **Zero spring cost.** Everything from Section 1 onwards sits on one red, so the split costs no
   spring change.

## 3. Warm-up — 5.5 min · RR · footbar up *(2:00 → 7:30)*

Dynamic only, simple repeated language, minimal correction, intensity building. All three planes
of motion are covered here: sagittal throughout, transverse in the Pilates V, frontal in the ball
squeeze that runs through the whole bridge sequence. The ball is in the hands for the footwork. That takes the arms off
the frame, so the trunk has to stabilise on its own. The one hold (top of the bridge with the ball
squeezed) is short and working. It isn't a stretch, so it still counts as dynamic.

**Order of the footwork.** Two legs to one, then feet to hips.

1. **Pilates V** opens the class. Balls of the feet, heels together. It switches on the adductors and
   deep hip rotators from the first rep.
2. **Toes, parallel.** The feet come back to hip-width, toes on the footbar, for the leg press.
   Then, in the same foot position, the ankles move on their own (Heel Raises) and get quick
   (Prances).
3. **Balls of the feet.** The feet settle onto the balls of the feet for a double leg press with
   both legs, then stay there as it goes to one leg (single leg extension), the peak of the legs.
4. **Heels on the footbar.** The bridges close the block: find neutral, articulate with the ball
   squeezed at the top, then a small lift and lower, then bridge 2 — press the carriage out and
   return — and finish parked with pulses, a 10% lift and a hold. The ball moves only once, from
   the arms to the knees.
5. **Roll Up.** The ball goes down and the spine articulates. The last one stays seated, so the
   room is already sitting up for the spring change.

| Exercise | Layering | Plane | Reps | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Leg Press — Pilates V** — balls of the feet, **heels touching, toes apart**, **ball held in the arms** | 8 presses out and in → 8 small **pulses** near straight legs (knees soft) | **Transverse** (hip ER) + sagittal | 8 + 8 | 0.75 min |
| **Leg Press, toes** — toes on the footbar, parallel, hip-width, ball in the arms | 8 full presses → 8 small **pulses** near straight legs | Sagittal | 8 + 8 | 0.75 min |
| **Heel Raises** — same foot position, legs straight, **holding the ball** | 6 full-range, then 6 small pulses at the top | Sagittal | 6 + 6 | 0.5 min |
| **Prances** — **holding the ball** | Slow and even → quicker and lighter | Sagittal | 20 alternating | 0.75 min |
| **Double Leg Press, balls of the feet** — parallel, hip-width, **ball held in the arms** | 8 full presses, slow return. *Progression:* 3 counts out, 3 counts home | Sagittal | 8 | 0.5 min |
| **Single Leg Extension** — one foot stays on the footbar, other leg to tabletop, **find neutral first** | *Option 1:* press out and **thread the ball under the tabletop leg**, hand to hand, knee stays at tabletop. *Option 2:* **extend the leg long over the footbar as the carriage comes in**, bend it back to tabletop as you press away — flexed toes, heel pressing into the bar. *Regression:* knee slightly past tabletop if the ball won't thread | Sagittal (+ frontal demand for pelvic control) | 6 / side | 1 min |
| **Glute Bridge** — heels on the footbar, **ball between the knees**, arms long | **Find neutral first** (tuck, arch, settle in the middle, a few ball squeezes there) → 3 articulating, **squeezing the ball at the top and holding the squeeze all the way down** → 8 small **lift and lower**, squeezing each lift | Sagittal → **frontal** (adduction) | neutral + 3 + 8 | 1 min |
| **Glute Bridge 2, press out & return** — from the top of the bridge | Press the carriage away and pull it back in; the hips lower an inch or two on the way out and lift on the way in, ball squeezed throughout. Then **park the carriage and finish**: 6 small pulses → hold the squeeze, lower 10% and lift ×6 → hold, breath in, roll down. *Regression:* small press, or lower the hips between reps. *Progression:* slower return | Sagittal | 6 + 6 + 6 | 0.75 min |
| **Pilates Roll Up** — ball down, legs long; **the last one stays seated** for the spring change | Articulate up and down through the spine. *Regression:* bent knees, hands behind the thighs, or a half roll-back. *Progression:* slower down phase. **No roll up for pregnancy, low back or osteoporosis** — roll to the side and press up to sitting | Sagittal (spinal flexion) | 4 | 0.5 min |

**Transition out:** after the last press-out, return the carriage, roll down and set the ball beside
the carriage. The last Roll Up finishes seated, and the spring change happens there. The room changes its own spring, so call
it from your own reformer so everyone can see the colour, then walk and check. Then everyone rolls
back down and takes the straps. **The footbar stays up** because Section 1 extends the legs over
and under it. One stop, not two.

> Pre-cue: "Last roll up — stay sitting at the top.
> Spring change: take one red off. One red only, and that's the only change today. Footbar stays
> up. Then roll back down and take your straps, and I'll check as I come round."

## 4. Section 1 — Core, hands in straps — 7 min · R *(the only spring change)* · footbar up *(7:30 → 14:30)*

| Exercise | Layering | Plane | Reps | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Arm Press Down** — arms long above the shoulders, press down to the hips and float back | Head stays down. *Regression:* feet flat on the carriage. *Progression:* legs to tabletop | Sagittal | 8 | 0.5 min |
| **Abdominal Curl** — press the arms down and curl the head and chest up on the same breath | Arms and curl move together, arms stay long. *Regression:* feet on the carriage, head down, press only. *Progression:* legs to tabletop | Sagittal (spinal flexion) | 8 | 0.5 min |
| **Curl + Single Leg Extension → Double Leg Extension** | Curl and hold. Extend **one leg** at a time, 6 each side, then extend **both legs together**, 6. *Regression:* head down, or single leg only. *Progression:* lower the legs | Sagittal | 6 / side → 6 double | 1.5 min |
| **Set 1 — over → under the footbar → hold → legs only → Scissors → return** | Curl + **Double Leg Extension over the footbar** ×4, then **Double Leg Extension under the footbar** ×4 → **hold** the extension → keep the arms and curl still and move **the legs only**, bend and extend ×4 → **Scissors, all the way up and all the way down** ×4/side → bend the knees and **return** to the start. *Regression:* stay over the bar, head down, smaller scissor | Sagittal (+ hip dissociation) | 4 over + 4 under + hold + 4 legs + 4 / side | 1.75 min |
| **Set 2 — extend → hold → Tricep Press → hold → Flutter Kick → return** | Curl + **Double Leg Extension** → **hold** the legs long → **Tricep Press** in the hold, elbows bend and straighten with the upper arms still ×6 → **hold** again → **Flutter Kick** the legs, small and quick, 20 beats → **return**. *Regression:* head down, legs higher, or tricep press with the feet on the carriage. *Progression:* lower the legs, slower tricep press | Sagittal (+ elbow extension under load) | Hold + 6 + 20 beats | 1.75 min |
| **Hundred** | *Regression:* legs to tabletop or head down. *Progression:* legs to 45° | Sagittal + breath | 100 beats | 1 min |

The section opens with the Arm Press Down: a simple, sagittal shoulder movement that lets everyone
find the straps, the spring and a stable ribcage with the head still down. The Abdominal Curl then
adds the curl to that same press, so the first two exercises are one movement built in two steps —
arms, then arms plus spine. There is no rotation and no arm circle here. The neck is loaded for the
whole section, so the time goes into the curl and the legs rather than into a second shape, and the
transverse plane is already covered in the Pilates V (§3), the Flying Lunge (§6) and the rows (§9).

From there the legs come in by degrees: one leg, then two legs, then two legs with the footbar as a
target. Over the bar is higher and easier on the low back, under the bar is lower and harder, so the
layering is visible without much explanation. Set 1 and Set 2 are the same shape — curl, extend,
hold — and the hold is where each set adds its own layer. Set 1 adds movement at the **hips**
(legs only, then the full scissor); Set 2 adds movement at the **arms** (the tricep press) and then
a faster, smaller leg beat. Each set ends with a deliberate **return**, which gives the neck a break
before the next one and stops the whole thing turning into one long hold. Set 2's flutter kick runs
straight into the Hundred.

**Transition out:** roll up to seated, step off to the side. No spring change and no bar change —
say so out loud ("same spring, one red") so nobody reaches for a spring out of habit.

## 5. Section 2a — Legs, FIRST SIDE — 5.5 min · R *(carried)* · footbar up *(14:30 → 20:00)*

Run all four on one side, then leave it. The second side is §7. The ball stays in the arms through
the Long Lunge and the Scooter. Put it down before the Flying Lunge, because the hands need the bar.

| Exercise | Layering | Plane | Reps | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Long Lunge** — **ball held in the arms** | Long lunge, ball still at the chest (no reach) → **long lunge + reach the ball up** → **long lunge, 1 ball pass** under the front thigh, **return** → **lunge hold, ball passing only**. *Regression:* shorter range, hold the ball still | Sagittal | 8 + 8 + 6 + 8 passes | 2 min |
| **Scooter** — **ball held in the arms** | Scooter → **1 scooter, 1 standing-heel raise** → last one, **hold the scooter (back leg long), heel raises only**. *Regression:* ball down, hands on the bar; heel stays down | Sagittal | 8 + 8 + 8 | 1.25 min |
| **Flying Lunge — knee tuck** — **ball down**, hands on the bar | *Regression:* foot stays on the ground. *Progression:* full tuck to full extension | Sagittal | 10 | 1 min |
| **Flying Lunge — knee left / right / centre** | Direct progression of the tuck, zero transition | **Transverse/frontal** | 6 cycles | 1 min |

Four exercises in 5.5 minutes — the extra sets and the holds (the manual's "pulses and holds"
variation) fill the time rather than a fifth exercise. Allow about 15 seconds across the block for
set-up between exercises.

**Transition out:** foot down, step onto the carriage and kneel, then pick up the ball and set it
between the knees for Down Stretch. No spring change, no bar change, keep talking.

---

## 6. Section 3 — Core, plank family — 6 min · R *(carried)* · footbar up *(20:00 → 26:00)*

Five consecutive wrist-loaded exercises. Offer the fists/forearms option **before** the first one,
not after someone is already uncomfortable. **The ball stays between the knees** from Down Stretch
through the push-up. The squeeze switches on the adductors and the deep core, and it keeps the legs
together as one line. It comes out for both Elephants.

| Exercise | Layering | Plane | Reps | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Down Stretch** — ball between the knees | *Regression:* smaller extension, chest lifted less. *Progression:* full range, longer carriage travel | Sagittal (extension) | 8 | 1.25 min |
| **Long Stretch** — ball between the knees | *Regression:* knees down. *Progression:* full plank, longer press | Sagittal | 6 | 1.25 min |
| **Push-up** — ball between the knees | Set the plank, hold 3 breaths, then press. *Regression:* knees down. *Progression:* slower lowering | Sagittal | 3 breaths + 8 | 1.25 min |
| **Elephant** — ball down | *Regression:* soft knees, smaller carriage travel. *Progression:* heels down, longer range | Sagittal | 8 | 1 min |
| **One-Leg Elephant stretch** — ball down | One leg lifts behind while the standing leg stretches; shoulders stay square and level, no hitching on the lifted-leg side. *Regression:* lifted foot stays low. *Progression:* longer carriage travel | Sagittal (+ frontal demand for pelvic control) | 5 / side | 1.25 min |

Down Stretch is the only spinal extension in the class. It now opens the block, so the spine is
lengthened before the flexion-heavy plank work. Don't cut it.

**Transition out:** from standing at the bar after the One-Leg Elephant, pick the ball up and set
up for the second leg side.

> Pre-cue: "Step off to the side — back to the legs, other side this time. Same springs, nothing
> to change."

---

## 7. Section 2b — Legs, SECOND SIDE — 5.5 min · R *(carried)* · footbar up *(26:00 → 31:30)*

Repeat §5 in full on the second side, same reps, same layers.

**Teach this side differently.** The exercises are already known, so cut the set-up cueing roughly
in half and spend the recovered time walking, observing and adjusting. This is your single best
window for the *Observation*, *Hands on adjustments* and *Active teaching* criteria — five and a
half minutes where nobody needs you to explain anything.

**Transition out:** from the Flying Lunge, step off to the side. Bar down, spring stays on one
red. Then sit or kneel facing the straps and cross them.

---

## 8. Section 4 — Back chain & arms — 8 min · R *(carried)* · footbar down *(31:30 → 39:30)*

Seated or kneeling facing the straps. **Cross the straps** for the three rows, then **uncross**
them for everything after. Crossing and uncrossing the straps is not a spring change, so it costs
nothing from the budget. The first two **optional** exercises sit straight after the uncross, while
everyone is still in the straps. The Bicep Curl is the last strap exercise, and the **optional
Mountain Climber** closes the block. It's the only exercise here without the straps, and it stays
facing the straps like everything else, so nobody turns around. All three optional exercises are
your time buffer, so if you're behind you can drop them without changing the shape of the block.

**Working on one red.** Arm work on a red is on the heavy side, and the shoulders have already done
Section 1 and the plank block. Set the load with position, not springs: **sit closer to the straps**
(less tension from the start) and move further back only if the rows feel easy. The heaviest
moments are the single-arm row, the Reverse Fly and the overhead press, so each has a range-based
regression below. Watch for shoulders lifting to the ears. That's the sign to shorten the range.

| Exercise | Straps | Layering | Plane | Reps | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Low Row** | Crossed | *Regression:* smaller range. *Progression:* pause at end range | Sagittal | 10 | 1.25 min |
| **High Row** | Crossed | Elbows lift to shoulder height. *Regression:* elbows lower, smaller range, sit closer. *Progression:* pause at end range | Transverse (horizontal abduction) | 10 | 1.25 min |
| **Single-Arm Row with Rotation** | Crossed | Row one arm, and let the ribcage turn to follow it. *Regression:* sit closer, smaller rotation, or row without the rotation. *Progression:* free hand reaches the opposite way | **Transverse** | 8 / side | 1.75 min |
| *Optional:* **Shoulder Retractions** — straight arms, draw back to the hips | **Uncross** | Light and instructional; blades down and back | Sagittal | 8 | 0.5 min |
| *Optional:* **Reverse Fly, open out** (→ V layer) | Uncrossed | Open-out is the base; the overhead V is the progression. *Regression:* smaller opening, sit closer; skip it with a shoulder flag | **Frontal** | 8 | 1 min |
| **Bicep Curl → hold → Press Up** | Uncrossed | Light and rhythmic curls, then **hold the last curl**, then **press the arms up overhead** from the hold. *Regression:* small press to forehead height, or skip the press and keep the curls. *Progression:* longer hold before the press | Sagittal | 10 + hold + 6 presses | 1.25 min |
| *Optional:* **Mountain Climber** — straps down, **still facing the straps** | — | Plank with the hands on the frame at the strap end and the feet on the carriage. One red gives a steady carriage. Draw both knees in towards the chest and press back out to a long plank. *Regression:* knees down (kneeling), smaller range. *Progression:* alternate — one knee drives in at a time, quicker tempo | Sagittal | 10 | 1 min |

*If you skip all three optional exercises* you reach the cooldown about 2½ minutes early. Spend that
time on longer cooldown holds (8 breaths instead of 5). Don't add new exercises.

**Transition into the Mountain Climber:** after the press up, put the straps away. Everyone stays
facing the straps.
> Pre-cue: "Last press — hold it up there. Now put the straps away and come onto your hands on the
> frame, feet on the carriage, still facing this way. Footbar stays down, same spring."

The Mountain Climber is the sixth wrist-loaded exercise in the class. Offer fists again before it
starts, and a knee pad for anyone taking the kneeling version.

---

## 9. Cooldown — 4.5 min · R *(carried)* · footbar down *(39:30 → 44:00)*

Static holds only, 5 breaths each, calm voice, minimal cueing, music noticeably down.
The sequence descends seated → kneeling → supine in one direction, so nobody has to get back up.

| Exercise | Note | Hold |
|---|---|---|
| **Mermaid** | Side-seated, reach up and over | 5 breaths / side |
| **Kneeling hip flexor stretch → Quad stretch** | The quad stretch is the same position with the back foot caught — offer it as a layer, not a separate set-up | 5 breaths / side |
| **Figure 4** | Supine. Use the strap or the bar to draw the leg in | 5 breaths / side |
| **Final twist** | Knees drop side to side, arms wide | 5 breaths / side |
| **Final breath** | Long, still, hands on the ribs | 20 sec |

This is the section most likely to get eaten if you run late, which is exactly why it's protected:
"included a cooldown that provided a slow end to class" is its own scored criterion, and a rushed
cooldown reads worse than a short one. If you arrive at 40:30 instead of 39:30, drop the final
twist — don't speed up the rest.

---

## 10. Class outro — 1 min *(44:00 → 45:00)*

> "Beautiful work — that was a big one in forty-five minutes. Take one more breath and notice how
> different your body feels from when you walked in. If anything felt off, or you want more or less
> of something next time, come and find me — I'd genuinely rather know. Thanks for moving with me."

Then: stand by the door, make eye contact as people leave, and check in individually with anyone
who flagged an injury at the start. The screening at the top is only worth marks if you close the
loop on it at the end.

## 11. Springs and apparatus

**One spring change, well under the maximum of three in AGENTS.md §5.2:**

`RR` (warm-up) → `R` (everything else: core/straps, legs, plank block, back, cooldown)

**The one call, word for word:**

| Change | Clock | Call | Bar |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7:30 | "Take one red off — one red only, and that's the only change today. Footbar stays up." | Up |

**One bar change**, at 31:30 (bar down entering Section 4), with no spring change attached. The footbar
stays up from the warm-up through Section 3, because Section 1 uses it as the target for the leg
extensions.

**Trade-offs worth naming** (an assessor rewards a stated trade-off and marks down an unexplained
one):

- **One change is a deliberate choice.** Every minute not spent on springs goes on movement, and
  the class never stops for set-up after 7:30. The load is managed with range and position instead
  of springs. That's the answer if an assessor asks why there's only one change.
- **The warm-up runs on two reds, not three.** Lighter than a heavy footwork setting, and that's
  the point: the return phase has to be controlled rather than caught by the spring, which is
  exactly the "the way back is the work" cue the warm-up is built around. It still carries the
  bridges and the press-out comfortably, and it's a solid load for the single leg extension. Strong
  regulars can slow the return rather than add spring.
- **The only change is the easiest one there is.** RR → R is "take one red off" — nothing to fit,
  nothing to find, and it happens seated straight after the bridges.
- **Section 1 runs on one red, not a blue.** A solid setting for the Hundred, the arm press-down,
  the leg extensions and the tricep press in Set 2. It asks more of the shoulders and the back of
  the arm, so the regressions are smaller arm range and head down, not a lighter spring.
- **The legs and the plank block run on one red, which is the less stable setting.** A lighter
  spring means a looser carriage, so the standing work (Long Lunge with the ball, Scooter, Flying
  Lunge) is wobblier and asks more of the balance and the deep core. Regress with range, and let
  anyone who needs it put the ball down and use the bar, rather than adding spring. Adding spring
  would cost two more changes (on and off again).
- **The plank block carries the leg spring rather than taking its own.** One red makes Long Stretch
  and Down Stretch harder to control on the way home, and that is the point. Knees-down is the
  first regression for anyone who can't hold the line. It also keeps the split-leg structure free.
- **Section 4 stays on one red, which is heavy for arm work.** A blue would be more comfortable for
  the single-arm row, the Reverse Fly and the overhead press, but it would cost a second change.
  Instead, everyone sits closer to the straps, and those three exercises have range-based
  regressions (Reverse Fly and Press Up can be skipped). If the room is mostly beginners or several
  people flag shoulders, the fallback is a blue at 31:30, done with the bar change. That's still
  only two changes.
- **Direction of progression is not universal.** Heavier = harder for the pressing work (footwork,
  rows); lighter = harder for the balance and stability work (Flying Lunge, Scooter, Long Stretch).
  Be ready to say this if asked why the springs go *down* for the standing work.

**Props:** one **small soft ball per reformer**. It's held in the arms in the warm-up and in the
Long Lunge/Scooter, passed under the knee or thigh, squeezed between the knees in the Bridges
and the plank block. It isn't used in Section 4. Have it on the carriage before class starts. Also: shoulder-rest blocks; a knee pad or folded towel for Down Stretch
and the kneeling hip flexor stretch — offer it during screening, not mid-class; a wedge or cushion
on hand if anyone flags pregnancy.

## 12. Anatomy — cueing support

| Exercise | Muscles cued | Set-up cue | Execution cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leg Press — Pilates V, toes parallel, then balls of the feet | Quadriceps, glutes, hamstrings, gastrocnemius/soleus; the Pilates V adds adductors and deep hip rotators. Holding the ball adds shoulder stabilisers | "Balls of the feet, heels touching, toes apart, ball held up over the chest" → "Feet parallel, hip-width, still on the balls of the feet" | "Press away, and resist the carriage home — then small pulses, knees soft" |
| Heel Raises | Gastrocnemius, soleus | "Legs straight, carriage still, ball stays still in the hands" | "Lift the heels, lower slowly — the carriage doesn't move" |
| Prances | Calves, ankle mobility, alternating hip flexors | "Light on the balls of the feet" | "One heel drops as the other lifts — quick and light" |
| Single Leg Extension + ball thread | Quadriceps, glutes, hamstrings, calf; glute medius for pelvic control; obliques for the thread | "One foot stays on the bar, other leg to tabletop, ball in the hands — find neutral first" | "Press the carriage out and thread the ball under the tabletop leg — hips stay level, the hip doesn't grip" |
| Glute Bridge — neutral, articulate, lift & lower | Glute maximus, hamstrings, spinal erectors; the squeeze adds the adductors and pelvic floor | "Heels on the bar, hip-width, ball between the knees, arms long by your sides — tuck, arch, settle in the middle" | "Peel up one bone at a time, squeeze the ball at the top and hold the squeeze all the way down — ribs wrap towards the hips, don't thrust the pelvis" |
| Glute Bridge 2, press out & return + pulses & hold | Hamstrings and glute max holding the hips; quadriceps pressing; adductors on the squeeze; deep core against the moving carriage | "Top of the bridge, ball squeezed" | "Press the carriage away — hips lower an inch — pull it home and they lift. Then park it: pulse, lower ten percent and lift, hold, and roll down" |
| Arm Press Down in straps | Latissimus dorsi, teres major, triceps, lower trapezius; abdominals to hold the ribs | "Straps in hands, arms straight above the shoulders" | "Press long down to the hips, resist back up — ribs stay heavy" |
| Abdominal Curl in straps | Rectus abdominis, transversus; latissimus dorsi and triceps pressing the arms | "Straps in hands, arms above the shoulders, legs at tabletop" | "Press the arms to the hips and curl up on the same breath — arms stay long" |
| Curl + Single Leg Extension | Rectus abdominis, transversus, iliopsoas; glute medius and obliques holding the pelvis level | "Curl up and stay up, one leg at a time" | "One leg goes on a journey, the bent knee stays home — the pelvis doesn't rock" |

## 13. Pilates Principles — where each one is taught

| Principle | Where it lands | What you actually say |
|---|---|---|
| **Breath** | Hundred (five in, five out); every cooldown hold | "Breathe wide into the sides of the ribs" |
| **Concentration** | Sections 2a/2b unilateral work with the ball pass; Single-Arm Row with Rotation | "One side at a time — all your attention on this leg" |

## 15. Teaching delivery notes

**Holding the room.** The warm-up uses simple, repeated language on purpose — it frees you to make
eye contact instead of over-explaining. Learn two or three names in the first two minutes.

**Cueing and pre-cueing.** At this pace, pre-cueing is what keeps the class from stalling. Pre-cue
every position change one exercise early, especially the three big ones: lying → seated at the end
of Section 1 (and sitting up for the spring change after the bridges before it), putting the ball down before the Flying Lunge (and back between the knees for Down
Stretch), and the turn-around plus crossing the straps at the end of Section 2b. Because the room sets its own springs, call the one change by colour and count — "take
one red off, one red only" — demonstrate it on your own reformer, and don't start cueing again
until you've scanned the carriages. Directional language to keep consistent: "carriage goes away",
"carriage comes home".

**Alignment.** Three things to watch all class: hips level in Single Leg Extension and Scooter (and feet
flaring in the bridges), ribs
knitted in the Hundred and Long Stretch, shoulders away from the ears in the whole back block.
With the ball in the arms, also watch that the shoulders don't creep up to the ears to hold it.

**Observation.** Section 2b is your window — the exercises are known, so move and watch rather than
talk. Scan every set, not just the first rep.

**Hands-on adjustments.** Highest-value moments: pelvis level in the Scooter,
hip squareness in the Flying Lunge, hips staying still in the Single-Arm Row with Rotation, a light
hand at the sacrum in Bridges. Ask before you touch; get consent at screening.

**Demonstration.** Fully demonstrate three exercises only — you don't have time for more: Flying
Lunge (orientation is confusing from the floor), the Long Lunge ball pass (so the rhythm of
"1 lunge, 1 pass, return" is clear), and crossing the straps (so nobody rows with them set wrong). Mirror
everything else.

**Motivation and personality.** Sections 2a and 2b are the natural energy windows — humour and
encouragement belong there. Drop your tone noticeably at the Mermaid; the contrast is what makes
four minutes of cooldown feel like a proper cooldown.

**Creativity and flow.** The three moments that show deliberate sequencing rather than a list:
splitting the legs around the plank block, the Flying Lunge knee pattern growing out of the tuck in the same shape, and the ball
as one prop that builds from a pass (coordination) to a squeeze (adductors and core). If you're asked "why this order?", those are your three
answers.

- **No separate lying lower-body block.** The AGENTS.md §5.3 framework allocates 7 minutes to it;

## 17. If you're running over or under

**Over (most likely).** Take it out of Section 4, in this order: drop the optional Mountain Climber,
then the optional Reverse Fly, then the optional Shoulder Retractions, then Bicep Curl 10 → 8 and Single-Arm Row with Rotation
8 → 6 per side. That's up to about 3¼ minutes, and nobody notices. If you're
still over at 40:30, drop the final twist from the cooldown.

**Never cut:** the second leg side, Down Stretch, the screening, or the outro. Each is either a
safety issue, a scored item, or leaves the class visibly unbalanced.

**Under.** Keep all three optional exercises in, then add reps rather than exercises — the Long Lunge
ball-pass hold and Low Row both take another 4 reps
comfortably, and the cooldown holds stretch from 5 breaths to 8 without changing the shape of
anything.
