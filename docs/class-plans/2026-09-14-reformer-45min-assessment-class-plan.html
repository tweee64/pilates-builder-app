Why the legs are split

The lower-body block is split in half with the core / plank block between the two sides (Section 2a → Section 3 → Section 2b). Three reasons, worth saying out loud if the assessor asks:

  • Recovery for the shoulder girdle. Down Stretch, Long Stretch, push-up and the two Elephants are five consecutive wrist- and shoulder-loaded exercises, and Section 4 is eight minutes of pulling. The second leg side gives the upper body a real break before it has to row.
  • Zero transition cost. Every junction matches the position either side of it: Flying Lunge (hands on bar) → Down Stretch (kneeling, hands on bar), then One-Leg Elephant (standing, hands on bar) → Long Lunge (standing). Nothing is jolted.
  • Zero spring cost. Everything from Section 1 onwards sits on one red, so the split spends none of the change budget.

Warm-up — footwork and bridges: 5.5 min

Spring: RR (footbar up)

Lying supine – feet on the footbar, hip-width, ball held in the arms for the footwork. Dynamic only, no static holds. All three planes: sagittal throughout, transverse in the Pilates V, frontal in the ball squeeze that runs through the bridges. Order: two legs to one, then feet to hips — the ball moves only once, from the arms to the knees.

  • Leg Press – Pilates V, balls of the feet, heels touching, toes apart, ball held over the chest x 8 (transverse + sagittal)
    • Small pulses near straight legs, knees soft x 8
  • Leg Press – toes on the bar, parallel, hip width, ball in the arms x 8
    • Small pulses near straight legs x 8
  • Heel Raises – same foot position, legs straight, holding the ball x 6 full range
    • Small pulses at the top x 6
  • Prances, holding the ball x 20 alternating – slow and even, then quicker and lighter
  • Double Leg Press – balls of the feet, parallel, hip width, ball in the arms x 8, slow return
    • 3 counts out, 3 counts home to advance
  • Single Leg Extension – one foot stays on the bar, other leg to tabletop, find neutral first x 6 each side
    • Option 1: press the carriage out and thread the ball under the tabletop leg, hand to hand – knee stays at tabletop
    • Option 2: extend the leg long over the footbar as the carriage comes in, bend the knee back as you press away – flexed toes, heel pressing into the bar
    • Knee slightly past tabletop if the ball won’t thread; don’t let the hip grip
  • Glute Bridge – heels on the footbar, ball between the knees, arms long
    • Find neutral first: tuck, arch, settle in the middle, a few ball squeezes there
    • Articulating x 3 – squeeze the ball at the top and hold the squeeze all the way back down to neutral; ribs wrap towards the hips, no pelvic thrust
    • Small lift and lower x 8, squeezing the ball each lift – glutes and hamstrings keep the carriage parked
  • Glute Bridge 2, press out & return – hips high, press the carriage away and pull it back in x 6
    • Hips lower an inch or two as the carriage goes out, lift as it comes in; ball squeezed throughout, knees tracking over the middle toes
    • Then park the carriage: small pulses x 6 → hold the squeeze, lower 10% and lift x 6 → hold, breath in, roll down
    • Small press, or lower the hips between reps to regress · slower return to advance
  • Pilates Roll Up – ball down, legs long x 4, the last one stays seated
    • Bent knees with hands behind the thighs, or a half roll-back, to regress · slower down phase to advance
    • No roll up for pregnancy, low back or osteoporosis – roll to the side and press up to sitting

The last Roll Up finishes seated – the spring change happens there. The room changes its own spring, so call it from your own reformer and let everyone see the colour, then walk and check. Footbar stays up, because Section 1 extends the legs over and under it. One stop, not two.

Pre-cue: “Last roll up — stay sitting at the top. Spring change: take one red off. One red only, and that’s the only change today. Footbar stays up. Then roll back down and take your straps, and I’ll check as I come round.”

Section 1 — Core, hands in straps: 7 min

Spring: R (the only change — footbar up)

Supine, straps in hands, arms above the shoulders. One red asks more of the shoulders than a blue — regress with a smaller arm range and head down, not a lighter spring.

  • Arm Press Down x 8 – head stays down, this one is just the arms
    • Feet flat on the carriage to regress · legs to tabletop to advance
  • Abdominal Curl x 8 – press the arms down and curl the head and chest up on the same breath
    • Arms and curl move together, arms stay long, no bending at the elbow
    • Feet on the carriage, head down, press only, to regress · legs to tabletop to advance
  • Curl + Single Leg Extension x 6 each side → Double Leg Extension x 6
    • Curl and hold; extend one leg at a time, then both together. Only the leg travels – the pelvis doesn’t rock
    • Head down, or single leg only, to regress · lower the legs to advance
  • Set 1 – over → under the bar → hold → legs only → Scissors → return
    • Curl + Double Leg Extension over the footbar x 4 – higher, kinder on the low back
    • Double Leg Extension under the footbar x 4 – lower, harder work
    • Hold the extension, then legs only – bend and extend x 4, arms and curl stay still
    • Scissors, all the way up and all the way down x 4 each side, then bend the knees and return
    • Stay over the bar, head down, smaller scissor to regress
  • Set 2 – extend → hold → Tricep Press → hold → Flutter Kick → return
    • Curl + Double Leg Extension, hold. Tricep Press in the hold x 6 – elbows bend and straighten, upper arms still, elbows pointing to the footbar
    • Hold again, then Flutter Kick x 20 beats – small and quick, the flutter is at the hips
    • Head down, legs higher, or tricep press with the feet on the carriage to regress · lower legs, slower tricep press to advance
  • Hundred x 100 beats – straight in from the flutter kick, no rest
    • Legs to tabletop or head down to regress · legs to 45° to advance

The section opens with the Arm Press Down: a simple sagittal shoulder movement that lets everyone find the straps, the spring and a stable ribcage with the head still down. The Abdominal Curl adds the curl to that same press, so the first two exercises are one movement built in two steps — arms, then arms plus spine. No rotation and no arm circles: the neck is loaded the whole way through, so the time goes into the curl and the legs, and the transverse plane is already covered in the Pilates V, the Flying Lunge and the rows.

Set 1 and Set 2 are the same shape — curl, extend, hold — and the hold is where each set adds its layer. Set 1 adds movement at the hips (legs only, then the full scissor); Set 2 adds it at the arms (the tricep press) and then a faster, smaller leg beat. Each set ends with a deliberate return, which gives the neck a break and stops the whole thing turning into one long hold.

Roll up to seated, step off to the side. No spring change, no bar change — say “same spring, one red” out loud so nobody reaches for a spring out of habit.

Section 2a — Legs, first side: 5.5 min

Spring: R (unchanged — footbar up)

Standing foot on the floor next to the carriage, back foot against the shoulder rest. Run all four on one side, then leave it — the second side is Section 2b. Ball in the arms for the Long Lunge and Scooter; put it down before the Flying Lunge.

  • Long Lunge, ball held at the chest, no reach x 8
    • Lunge + reach the ball up overhead x 8
    • 1 lunge, 1 ball pass under the front thigh, return x 6
    • Lunge hold, ball passing only x 8
    • Shorter range, back knee resting on the carriage, or hold the ball still, to regress
  • Scooter, ball held at the chest, pressing straight back x 8
    • Press back + 1 standing-heel raise each rep x 8
    • Hold the back leg long, heel raises only x 8
    • Ball down, hands on the bar, heel stays down to regress
  • Flying Lunge — knee tuck, ball down, hands on the bar x 10
    • Foot stays on the ground to regress · lift the flying leg higher to advance
  • Flying Lunge — knee left / right / centre x 6 cycles (transverse / frontal)
    • A direct progression of the tuck — same shape, zero transition

Four exercises in 5.5 minutes — the extra sets and the holds fill the time rather than a fifth exercise. Allow about 15 seconds across the block for set-up between exercises. One red is the looser, less stable setting, so the standing work is wobblier and asks more of balance and deep core: regress with range, or ball down and hands on the bar — never by adding spring.

Foot down, step onto the carriage and kneel, then pick up the ball and set it between the knees for Down Stretch. No spring change, no bar change, keep talking.

Section 3 — Core, plank family: 6 min

Spring: R (unchanged — footbar up)

Five consecutive wrist- and shoulder-loaded exercises. Offer fists or forearms before the first one, not after someone is uncomfortable. The ball stays between the knees from Down Stretch through the push-up — the squeeze switches on the adductors and deep core and keeps the legs as one line — and comes out for both Elephants.

  • Down Stretch, ball between the knees x 8
    • Smaller extension, chest lifted less to regress · full range, longer carriage travel to advance
  • Long Stretch, ball between the knees x 6 – straight out of Down Stretch, no re-set
    • Knees down to regress (that is Down Stretch, and it is the same work) · longer press to advance
  • Plank, ball between the knees, hold x 3 breaths → Push-up x 8
    • Knees down to regress · slower lowering to advance
  • Elephant, ball down x 8
    • Soft knees, smaller carriage travel to regress · heels down, longer range to advance
  • One-Leg Elephant stretch, ball down, spine neutral x 5 each side
    • Shoulders square and level — no hitching up on the lifted-leg side
    • Lifted foot stays low to regress · longer carriage travel to advance

Down Stretch is the only spinal extension in the class. It opens the block, so the spine is lengthened before the flexion-heavy plank work. Do not cut it.

From standing at the bar after the One-Leg Elephant, pick the ball up and set up for the second leg side.

Pre-cue: “Step off to the side — back to the legs, other side this time. Same springs, nothing to change.”

Section 4 — Back chain and arms: 8 min

Spring: R (unchanged — footbar down, the only bar change)

High kneeling facing the straps for the whole block, ball held high between the thighs — the squeeze squares the pelvis, stops the hips swinging in the rows, and is what lets the single-arm rotation stay in the spine instead of the hips. Ropes crossed for the three rows, then uncrossed for everything after — not a spring change. Cross the ropes, not the hands, and hold down at the silver knots.

  • Low Row – the narrow row, ropes crossed x 10
    • Contract and resist, not contract and release — the return isn’t a rest; don’t flare the ribs
    • Smaller range to regress · pause at end range to advance
  • High Row – the wide row, elbows just below shoulder height x 10 (transverse)
    • Elbows lower, smaller range, sit closer to regress · pause at end range to advance
  • Single-Arm Row with Rotation, ropes crossed x 8 each side (transverse)
    • High or low, your choice; the ribs turn, the hips stay facing forward — that is what the ball is for
    • Sit closer, smaller rotation, or no rotation to regress · free hand reaches the opposite way to advance
  • Optional: Shoulder Retractions, ropes uncrossed, straight arms drawing back past the hips x 8
  • Optional: Reverse Fly, open out x 8 (frontal)
    • Smaller opening, sit closer to regress; skip it with a shoulder flag
    • Palms down and lift into a slight V to advance — open-out is a complete exercise on its own
  • Bicep Curl, ropes uncrossed x 10 – elbows lifted just below the shoulders
    • Hold the last curl, then press the arms up overhead x 6
    • Small press to forehead height, or skip the press, to regress · shuffle further from the shoulder rests to advance
  • Optional: Mountain Climber, straps away, still facing the straps x 10
    • Plank, hands on the frame at the strap end, feet on the carriage – draw both knees in, press back out to a long plank
    • Knees down (kneeling), smaller range to regress · one knee at a time, quicker tempo to advance

One red matches the manual’s setting for rows but it is heavy for arm work after Section 1 and the plank block. Set the load with position, not springs: everyone sits closer to the straps and moves back only if the rows feel easy. The heaviest moments are the single-arm row, the Reverse Fly and the overhead press. Shoulders lifting to the ears = shorten the range.

The Mountain Climber is the sixth wrist-loaded exercise — offer fists again before it starts, and a knee pad for anyone taking the kneeling version. All three optionals are your time buffer: skip them all and you are ~2½ minutes early, so hold the cooldown for 8 breaths instead of 5 rather than adding exercises.

Pre-cue: “Last press — hold it up there. Now put the straps away and come onto your hands on the frame, feet on the carriage, still facing this way. Footbar stays down, same spring.”

Cooldown: 4.5 min

Spring: R (unchanged — footbar down)

Static holds only, 5 breaths each, calm voice, minimal cueing, music noticeably down. The sequence descends seated → kneeling → supine, so nobody has to get back up. No spring change for the cooldown — we stay on one red.

  • Mermaid, side-seated, reach up and over x 5 breaths each side
  • Hip opener → quad stretch, kneeling, x 5 breaths each side
    • Tuck the tailbone first, then sink the hips forward — without the tuck you just arch the back
    • The quad stretch is the same position with the back foot caught: a layer, not a new set-up
  • Supine Figure 4, hands behind the thigh or use the strap, x 5 breaths each side
  • Final twist, knees side to side, arms wide, x 5 breaths each side
  • Final breath – long, still, hands on the ribs x 20 sec

“Included a cooldown that provided a slow end to class” is its own scored criterion, and a rushed cooldown reads worse than a short one — which is exactly why it is protected. If you arrive here at 40:30 instead of 39:30, drop the final twist. Do not speed up the rest.

Class outro: 1 min

“Beautiful work — that was a big one in forty-five minutes. Take one more breath and notice how different your body feels from when you walked in. If anything felt off, or you want more or less of something next time, come and find me — I’d genuinely rather know. Thanks for moving with me.”

Then stand by the door, make eye contact as people leave, and check in individually — by name — with anyone who flagged an injury at the start. The screening only scores if you close the loop on it at the end.

Trade-offs worth naming — an assessor rewards a stated trade-off and marks down an unexplained one:

  • One change is a deliberate choice. Every minute not spent on springs goes on movement, and the class never stops for set-up after 7:30. The load is managed with range and position instead of springs. That is the answer if an assessor asks why there is only one change.
  • The warm-up runs on two reds, not three. Lighter than a heavy footwork setting, and that is the point: the return has to be controlled rather than caught by the spring — “the way back is the work”, which is the cue the whole warm-up is built around. It still carries the bridges and the press-out comfortably, and it is a solid load for the single leg extension. Strong regulars slow the return rather than add spring.
  • The only change is the easiest one there is. RR → R is “take one red off” — nothing to fit, nothing to find, and it happens seated straight after the Roll Up.
  • Section 1 runs on one red, not a blue. A solid setting for the Hundred, the arm press-down, the leg extensions and the tricep press. It asks more of the shoulders and the back of the arm, so the regressions are smaller arm range and head down, not a lighter spring.
  • The legs and the plank block run on one red, the less stable setting. A looser carriage makes the standing work (Long Lunge with the ball, Scooter, Flying Lunge) wobblier and asks more of balance and deep core. Regress with range, or ball down and hands on the bar — not by adding spring, which would cost two more changes (on and off again).
  • The plank block carries the leg spring rather than taking its own. One red makes Long Stretch and Down Stretch harder to control on the way home, and that is the point. Knees down is the first regression, and it keeps the split-leg structure free.
  • Section 4 stays on one red, which is heavy for arm work. A blue would be more comfortable for the single-arm row, the Reverse Fly and the overhead press, but it would cost a second change. Instead everyone sits closer to the straps, and those three have range-based regressions (the Reverse Fly and the Press Up can be skipped). If the room is mostly beginners or several people flag shoulders, the fallback is a blue at 31:30, done with the bar change — still only two changes.
  • Direction of progression is not universal. Heavier is harder for the pressing work (footwork, rows); lighter is harder for the balance and stability work (Flying Lunge, Scooter, Long Stretch). Be ready to say this if asked why the springs go down for the standing work.

Props. One small soft ball per reformer — held in the arms in the warm-up and the standing work, threaded under the tabletop leg or passed under the thigh, squeezed between the knees in the Bridges and the plank block, held high between the thighs through the back block. Have it on the carriage before class starts. Also: shoulder-rest blocks; a knee pad for Down Stretch, the high kneeling block and the hip opener — offer it at screening, not mid-class; a wedge if anyone flags pregnancy.

If you are running over or under

Over (most likely). Take it out of Section 4, in this order:

  • Drop the optional Mountain Climber
  • Drop the optional Reverse Fly
  • Drop the optional Shoulder Retractions
  • Bicep Curl 10 → 8
  • Single-Arm Row with Rotation 8 → 6 each side

That is up to about 3¼ minutes and nobody notices. If you are still over at 40:30, drop the final twist from the cooldown.

Never cut: the second leg side, Down Stretch, the screening, or the outro. Each is either a safety issue, a scored item, or leaves the class visibly unbalanced.

Under. Keep all three optional exercises in, then add reps rather than exercises — the Long Lunge ball-pass hold and the Low Row both take another 4 reps comfortably, and the cooldown holds stretch from 5 breaths to 8 without changing the shape of anything.

Teaching delivery notes

  • Holding the room. The warm-up uses simple, repeated language on purpose — it frees you to make eye contact instead of over-explaining. Learn two or three names in the first two minutes.
  • Cueing and pre-cueing. At this pace, pre-cueing is what keeps the class from stalling. Pre-cue every position change one exercise early, especially the three big ones: lying → seated at the end of Section 1 (and sitting up for the spring change at the top of the Roll Up before it), putting the ball down before the Flying Lunge (and back between the knees for Down Stretch), and the turn-around plus crossing the ropes at the end of Section 2b. Because the room sets its own springs, call the one change by colour and count — “take one red off, one red only” — demonstrate it on your own reformer, and don’t start cueing again until you’ve scanned the carriages. Keep the language consistent: “carriage goes away”, “carriage comes home”.
  • Alignment. Three things to watch all class: hips level in the Single Leg Extension and the Scooter (and feet flaring in the bridges), ribs knitted in the Hundred and Long Stretch, shoulders away from the ears in the whole back block. With the ball in the arms, also watch that the shoulders don’t creep up to hold it.
  • Observation. Section 2b is your window — the exercises are known, so move and watch rather than talk. Scan every set, not just the first rep.
  • Hands-on adjustments. Highest-value moments: pelvis level in the Scooter, hip squareness in the Flying Lunge, hips staying still in the Single-Arm Row with Rotation, a light hand at the sacrum in the Bridges. Ask before you touch; consent was taken at screening, so use their name.
  • Demonstration. Fully demonstrate three only — you don’t have time for more: the Flying Lunge (orientation is confusing from the floor), the Long Lunge ball pass (so the rhythm of “1 lunge, 1 pass, return” is clear), and crossing the ropes (so nobody rows with them set wrong). Mirror everything else.
  • Motivation and personality. Sections 2a and 2b are the natural energy windows — humour and encouragement belong there. Drop your tone noticeably at the Mermaid; the contrast is what makes four and a half minutes of cooldown feel like a proper cooldown.
  • Creativity and flow. Three moments that show deliberate sequencing rather than a list: splitting the legs around the plank block, the Flying Lunge knee pattern growing out of the tuck in the same shape, and the ball as one prop that builds — from a thread and a pass (coordination) in the warm-up and lunges, to a squeeze (adductors and deep core) in the bridges and plank block, to a squeeze held high between the thighs in the rows, where it is what keeps the pelvis square through the rotation. If asked “why this order?”, those are your three answers.

Deviations to own if asked

All deliberate, none accidental:

  • No separate lying lower-body block. The framework allocates 7 minutes to it; here it is covered by the Bridges in the warm-up, and the time is spent on standing lower body instead. The framework explicitly permits weaving muscle groups through the class rather than blocking them.
  • No dedicated adductor exercise. Inner-thigh work is covered by the ball squeeze in the Bridges, through the plank block and high between the thighs in the back block, plus the Pilates V Leg Press.
  • No prone work. Down Stretch carries all the spinal extension — a considered omission given how much plank loading is already in the class.
