**Checkpoints to memorise:** feet in straps by **21:00**, integration block starts at **36:00**,
cooldown starts at **52:00**. If I'm behind at any of them, cut reps in Section 4 first (the
Shoulder Retractions go, then a rep off each of the box exercises) — never in the cooldown, and
never in Section 3, because that's where the balance assessment lives.

**Spring logic, in one line:** heaviest at the front where the carriage should feel supportive and
predictable under a sensitive back and where the short box needs to stay parked (RRB), one red off
for the whole bridge and feet-in-straps block (RR, exactly what the manual gives for both), down to
a single red once the work becomes closed-chain and body-weight-led, and finishing light on blue
where the neck and shoulders are loaded.

**Footbar:** up for the warm-up, bridge and Section 3; down for feet in straps, hands in straps,
the long box and the cooldown. Three moves, each one landing on a transition they're making anyway.

## 3. The class plan

### 3.1 Pre-class — 6 min · no springs *(0:00 → 6:00)*

Not a warm-up — an assessment. I want to see them move before the machine gives them any support.

| Screen | What I do | What I'm looking for |
|---|---|---|
| **Standing posture** | Look from the side and behind. Feet, knees, pelvis, ribcage, shoulders, head | Pelvic tilt (ant/post), degree of thoracic kyphosis, head-forward position, shoulder height difference, foot/knee alignment |
| **Overhead reach ×3** | Arms up, palms in | How far before the ribs flare or the low back extends — this is my shoulder range answer |
| **Bodyweight squat ×3** | Feet hip width | Depth before the pelvis tucks; knee tracking on the affected side; whether they can sit back |
| **Single-leg stand, 10 sec / side** | Eyes open, hands free | Hip hitching, ankle strategy, left/right difference — the balance baseline |

Then onto the carriage, supine, heels on the footbar, carriage parked: **teach the breath and
neutral pelvis.** Pelvic tilts — tuck, arch, settle in the middle — then three breaths in neutral,
ribs heavy. Nothing else happens until this lands: it is the reference position for the whole hour
and the thing they have never been taught.

> "Everything we do today comes back to this shape. When I say 'find neutral,' this is what I mean."

Ask the back question before the first exercise and again in the outro: **"where's the back today,
nought to ten?"** Two numbers, an hour apart, is the most useful thing I'll write down.

## 6. Reflection

[Complete after the session. **Max 500 words** per the template — the draft below is ~480, so
there's room for the bracketed bits once they're filled in, but check the count before submitting.]

Three things to plan around at once: a back that flares up, a neck and shoulders that hold tension,
and a knee that doesn't like depth. None of them serious on its own, but together they made this
harder to plan than I expected, and my first version was far too full. I cut about a third of it.
In an assessment session I'm after information, and that comes from watching how someone moves, not
from how much I fit into the hour.

The decision I feel best about is putting the short box in the warm-up. The manual wants heavy
springs under the box for support, and my heaviest springs are already on for footwork, so it costs
me nothing in spring changes and it buys me a rotation test at nine minutes instead of never. I
used Lean rather than Abdominal Curls deliberately — the same trunk work without loading the spine
into flexion, which is the one thing this client has told me they don't like. Feet in straps then
came early, while they were still fresh, because for a back like this the Frog Press tells me more
than anything else in the plan: whether the deep abdominals can hold the pelvis neutral while the
legs lengthen and the load comes on. I also wrote down the exact height in Hamstring Press where
neutral gave way [what that was], so I have a number to work from instead of an impression.

Springs went heavy to light across the hour, RRB down to a single blue, and both of the middle
blocks sit on the manual's own recommendation — RR for Bridge and the whole feet-in-straps series,
so one change covers twelve minutes. Three changes is the maximum the manual allows and I used all
three, but each landed on a transition they were making anyway. The knee pad was out before we
started rather than fetched after they winced in the Scooter. Small thing, but I think it landed.

What I'd change is the middle of the session. Between 15:00 and 36:00 they're on their back for
twenty-one minutes straight, and by the Hundreds [the neck was / wasn't coping]. Next time I'd pull
the Elephant and Arabesque forward to break it up, and finish the supine work with the straps. I'd
