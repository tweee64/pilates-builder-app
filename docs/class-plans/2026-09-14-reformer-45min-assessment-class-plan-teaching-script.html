Teaching script

Reformer
Assessment Class

45 minutes · door to door
Level  Mixed level · Group reformer
Props  one small soft ball per reformer · shoulder-rest blocks · knee pad or folded towel · wedge on standby
Springs  RR → R — one change, one bar change. The room sets its own.
Quoted lines are what you say. Chips are what you do.

How to use this script

  • Lines in quotes are what you say. Say them in your own mouth-shape — don’t read them. If a sentence feels like someone else’s, cut it.
  • [DO] = what your hands and feet are doing: set-up, demo, spring calls, walking the room.
  • [SCAN] = the one or two things to look for. Not a list to recite — a thing to find on one or two bodies and fix by name.
  • [SILENCE] = stop talking. Deliberate. A class where the teacher never stops talking never lets anyone feel anything.
  • [ASSESSED] = a scored criterion lands here. Don’t perform it — just don’t skip it.
  • Count down, never up. “Three, two, one” tells people the end is real.
  • The one spring change is called by colour and count, demonstrated on your own reformer, then checked. One stop, not two. After 7:30, nothing on the springs moves again.
  • Cues follow the Reformer manual. Each exercise names the manual page its set-up, directional and alignment cues come from. Anything marked in-house isn’t in the manual.

0:00 — INTRODUCTION & SCREENING (2 min)

springs RR · footbar up
DOStand where the whole room can see you, not behind your own machine. Knee pads visible on the floor, a small ball on every carriage.
ASSESSEDIntroduction and screening. Keep it to two minutes but do not clip it — it’s a scored item and it’s the safety basis for everything after.

“Hi everyone, I’m Thuy — and today i’m going to walk you through 45 minutes of Reformer Pilates.”

“Quick check before we start: anyone brand new to Reformer? Anyone pregnant, or working around an injury — back, shoulder, wrist, knee, ankle? Just give me a show of hand, or I will also tell you the modification during the exercise”

“For anyone who’s new, some direction around the class, this is also the front of the reformer, the footbar direction,. This is the back of the reformer, shoulder block direction.”

For some direction, this is the forest side, and the window side of the reformer.

DOWait. Actually wait. Make eye contact with anyone new. Note who told you what — you’ll use it at the bridges, the leg extensions, the lunges and the plank block.

“A few things about how today runs.”

“ grab a small ball for your reformer — we’ll use it throughout the class.”

“There is a consent token at the top the reformer, if you’re comfortable with hands-on adjustment, flip it to yes. if not, i will try to adjust you verbally.”

“For today’s class, we’ll do a full body workout, with some leg exercises, some stretching, as well as some back exercises. Now, let’s get ready for warmup.”

“Springs to start: two reds, footbar up. Set that now and I’ll come round.”

DOWalk the room. Check every carriage. This is the last easy check of the class.

“Let’s take one breath together before we move. Inhale wide into the sides of the ribs… and exhale, everything out.”

SILENCEOne full breath. That’s the arrival.

2:00 — WARM-UP (5.5 min)

🔴🔴 RR · footbar up · ball in hands

Purpose: dynamic, simple repeated language, building intensity. All three planes live in this block. The ball is in the hands for the footwork — the arms come off the frame, so the trunk has to stabilise on its own. Order: Pilates V → toes → balls of the feet → one leg → heels for the bridges → Roll Up to sitting for the spring change.

ASSESSEDWarm-up. Simple language on purpose — it frees you to make eye contact instead of over-explaining. Learn two or three names in this block.
DOPregnancy flag: lying on the back only for a few minutes at a time, wedge from the second trimester, and less spring. Stop if there’s any dizziness.

Leg Press — Pilates V · 8 + 8 pulses · ~45 sec

Manual: Double Leg Press, p.101

“Lie all the way back. Toes on the footbar. Heels touching, toes apart — Pilates V. “

“Take your ball and hold it up over your chest. Arms stay still.”

“Wrap the backs of the legs together as you press. Exhale — straighten the legs and press the carriage away. Inhale — bend the knees and bring it home.”

“Control, not momentum. Soft knees at the end — don’t lock them. Even weight through both sides of the pelvis.”

“Eight of these. Three, two, one. Press all the way out… now bring the carriage back just halfway and stop there.”

“From here, small pulses in the top half — out towards straight, back to halfway. Don’t let it come any further home than that. Knees soft. Eight, seven, six, five, four, three, two, one.”

“Bend the knees, carriage all the way home.”

SCANHeels coming apart in the V, or knees rolling in. Cue: ”knees over the middle toes, heels stay touching.” And shoulders creeping up to hold the ball — ”ball is light, shoulders are heavy.” On the pulses, watch for the carriage creeping all the way home — ”stay in the top half.”

Leg Press — toes, parallel · 8 + 8 pulses · ~45 sec

Manual: Double Leg Press, p.101

“Feet to parallel, hip-width — toes on the footbar, heels lifted high.”

“Inhale to prepare, Exhale — press out. Inhale — bring it home. Heels stay lifted the whole way.”

“Eight. Three, two, one. Press out, and come back to halfway.”

“Same as before — pulse in the upper half. Little movements, heels still high. Eight, seven, six, five, four, three, two, one.”

“Carriage home. Stay on the toes.”

SCANHeels dropping as the carriage comes home. Cue: ”heels stay high, like standing on tiptoe.”

Heel Raises — 6 full + 6 pulses · ~30 sec

Manual: Heel Raises, p.102

“Press the carriage out — legs straight, knees soft. Ball stays still in the hands.”

“Inhale — let the heels roll under the footbar. Exhale — lift both heels high. Legs stay straight.”

“The only thing moving is the ankle. Fluid, not bouncy.”

“Even pressure through the big toe and the little toe — don’t roll out onto the edges of the feet.”

“Six. Three, two, one. Stay at the top — six small pulses. Six, five, four, three, two, one.”

“And enjoy the stretch as the heels lower under the bar.”

Prances — 20 alternating · ~45 sec

Manual: Prances, p.103

“Legs straight, still holding the ball.”

“Inhale — bend one knee and roll the other heel under the bar. Exhale — lift both heels high, legs straight. Then the other side.”

“Pause for a moment when both legs are long.”

“Fluid, not bouncy. Knees over feet, even through big toe and little toe.”

DOLet the music carry this one. Count only the last few.

“Ten more… and five, four, three, two, one. Bend the knees, carriage home.”

Double Leg Press — balls of the feet · 8 · ~30 sec

Manual: Double Leg Press, p.101

“Feet settle onto the balls of the feet — parallel, hip-width. Ball still over the chest.”

“Exhale — press away, and reach the ball up to the ceiling. Inhale — ball back over your chest, and take your time coming home. The way back is the work.”

“Press out, ball up. Come home, ball in.”

“More: three counts out, three counts home.”

“Eight. Three, two, one — and on the last one, press out, ball up, and stay there.”

Single Leg Extension + ball thread — 6 each side · ~1 min

Manual: Single Leg Press, p.104 · ball passed under the knee, full class plan p.233 · straight-leg-over-the-bar pattern in-house

DOStraight on from the double leg press — the carriage is already out. Teach option 1 first, add option 2 when the room has it. Side two goes straight in.

Set-up

“Left foot stays exactly where it is. Ball into your hands. Right leg up to tabletop — knee over the hip.”

“Find neutral — natural arch in the low back, pelvis grounded.”

Option 1 — thread the ball, knee stays at tabletop

“Inhale to prepare. Exhale — press the carriage all the way out with the left leg, and thread the ball straight under the right leg, hand to hand. Knee stays at tabletop.”

“Carriage home — ball comes back to your chest.”

“Struggling to thread it? Take the knee slightly past tabletop. Just don’t let the hip grip.”

Option 2 — add the leg

“Next time the carriage comes in, extend the right leg long over the footbar. Then bend the knee back to tabletop as you press the carriage away.”

“Flex the right toes and press the heel away — firm through the back of that leg.”

“Weight even through both sides of the pelvis. Left knee over the left hip.”

“Six. Three, two, one. Carriage home, both feet on the bar.”

“Other side — press out with both feet, then lift the left leg to tabletop. Six. Three, two, one.”

SCANAs it loads, people start to tuck under and overcompensate — cue ”ribs soft, find neutral again.” The pelvis tipping to chase the thread, and the hip of the tabletop leg gripping. A light hand on the hip is worth more than three sentences here.

Glute Bridge — neutral → 3 articulating → 8 lift & lower · ~1 min

Manual: Bridge 1, p.110 · bridge roll + ball squeeze, full class plan p.233 · pulses and hold in-house

Find neutral first

“Heels on the footbar, hip-width. Ball between the knees. Arms long by your sides.”

“Tuck… arch… and settle in the middle. That’s neutral. Squeeze the ball a few times while you’re there — inner thighs on, spine still. Remember that feeling.”

Articulate — 3

“Inhale. Exhale — tuck the tail and peel the spine off the carriage, bone by bone by bone.”

“At the top, wrap the ribs down towards the hips — don’t thrust the pelvis. Squeeze the ball — and hold the squeeze as you roll back down.”

“Ripple down — upper back, middle back, lower back. Find neutral at the bottom.”

“The carriage stays parked. Three of those.”

Lift & lower — 8

“Now smaller. Exhale — lift the hips and squeeze the ball. Inhale — lower.”

“Gentle lift and lower — this one is glutes and hamstrings. The hamstrings are what keep the bed parked.”

“Eight. Three, two, one — and this time stay up at the top.”

SCANKnees rolling out and feet flaring. Cue: ”knees over the middle toes.” The carriage creeping home — ”hamstrings hold the bed parked.” The neck jamming at the top — ”space between chin and chest.”

Glute Bridge 2 — press out & return — 6 · then pulses & hold · ~45 sec

Manual: Bridge 2, p.111 · pulses, 10% lift and hold in-house

“Stay high, keep the squeeze. Press the carriage away — that’s bridge 2 — then pull it back in and park the bed.”

“As the carriage presses out the hips lower an inch or two. As it comes in, they lift back up. The body stays in one long line.”

“Squeeze the ball the whole time — that’s what keeps the pelvis square. Watch the feet don’t roll out — knees tracking over the middle toes.”

“Six. Three, two, one — and on the last one, carriage all the way in, and park it there.”

“Squeeze the ball like you want to pop it in half. Little pulses — six, five, four, three, two, one.”

“Hold the squeeze — now lower about ten percent, and lift. Six, five, four, three, two, one.”

“And hold. Breath in… and slowly roll the spine all the way down.”

SILENCEOne breath in the hold.
SCANHips dropping as the carriage travels. Cue: ”hips stay level — only the legs move.”

Pilates Roll Up — 4, last one stays seated · ~30 sec

In-house · ball down, legs long · heavy springs keep the carriage still

DOPregnancy / low-back / osteoporosis flag: no roll up. They stay lying down, then roll onto their side and press up to sitting for the last one.

“Ball down beside you. Legs long over the footbar, feet together, flexed. Arms reach up to the ceiling.”

“Inhale — tuck your chin, and start to curl the head and shoulders up. Exhale — peel the spine off the carriage one bone at a time and reach forward over the legs.”

“Inhale — sit tall. Exhale — tuck the tailbone and roll back down, one bone at a time, the head is last.”

“The carriage stays parked. Belly scoops away from the thighs. Legs stay heavy.”

“Easier: bend the knees, hands behind the thighs, and walk yourself up — or just roll halfway back and return. More: take longer on the way down.”

“Four. Three, two… and last one — roll up and stay sitting at the top.”

SCANLegs lifting or feet popping up as they roll up. Cue: ”heels heavy — bend the knees if you need to.” Look for people pulling with the neck or throwing the arms. Cue: ”nod first, then peel.”
⚙THE ONLY SPRING CHANGE — RR → R. Footbar STAYS UP. Everyone is already seated from the last Roll Up. Call it from your own reformer so the room sees the colour.

“Stay sitting — spring change. Take one red off. One red only — and that’s the only change today. Footbar stays up — don’t touch it.”

“Then roll back down, shoulders just away from the shoulder rests, and take your short loops, and I’ll check as I come round.”

DOWalk. Scan every carriage. Do not start cueing again until the last one is set. This is one stop, not two.

7:30 — SECTION 1 · CORE, HANDS IN STRAPS (7 min)

🔴 R · footbar up (carried)

Purpose: a simple arm press down to settle the straps and the ribcage, then the same press with a curl on top — arms first, then arms plus spine. The legs come in one at a time, then together, and the footbar becomes the target. No rotation and no arm circles in this block: the neck is loaded the whole way through, so the time goes into the curl and the legs, and the transverse plane is covered in the Pilates V, the Flying Lunge and the rows. One red asks more of the shoulders than a blue — regress with a smaller arm range and head down, not a lighter spring.

DOPregnancy flag: no spinal flexion — head and shoulders stay down for every curl in this block, wedge under the shoulders. Neck flag: head rest up, head down. Low-back flag: if neutral isn’t possible, work in imprint and stay in the over-the-bar set only.

Arm Press Down — 8 · ~30 sec

Manual: Hands in Straps (arm press down)

“Hands in the straps, arms straight above the shoulders, palms facing the footbar. Legs lift one at a time to tabletop — or keep the feet down on the carriage.”

“Head stays down for these. This one is just the arms.”

“Exhale — press the arms long, all the way down to the hips. Inhale — resist them back up, only as far as above the shoulders.”

“Ribs stay heavy. The shoulders stay away from the ears.”

“Eight. Three, two, one. Arms back above the shoulders.”

SCANRibs flaring as the arms come back up. Cue: ”ribs wrapped in and down — stop the arms above the shoulders.”

Abdominal Curl — 8 · ~30 sec

Manual: Hands in Straps – Abdominal Curls, p.155

DOSame press as before, with the curl added on top. Say that out loud — it’s the same movement, one layer on.

“Same press. This time the head and chest come with it.”

“Inhale to prepare. Exhale — press the arms down to the hips AND curl the head and chest up. Inhale — lower the head, float the arms back above the shoulders.”

“Arms and curl move together, and the arms stay long. No bending at the elbow.”

“Lift from your abdominals, not your neck. Eyes gaze over the knees, space between chin and chest.”

“Easier: press only, head stays down, or feet down on the carriage. More: legs to tabletop.”

“Eight. Three, two, one. And head down.”

SCANChin jamming into the chest, or elbows bending to get the curl up. Cue: ”space between chin and chest — broaden across the chest, arms stay long.”

Curl + Single Leg Extension → Double Leg Extension — 6 each side, then 6 · ~1.5 min

Manual: Abdominal Curls, p.155 (single / double leg extension variation)

“Legs to tabletop. Arms long, reaching towards the footbar. Exhale — curl up, and stay up.”

“Now one leg at a time — extend the right leg long, draw it back to tabletop. Left leg extends, and back. The other knee stays exactly where it is.”

“Only the leg travels. The pelvis doesn’t rock.”

“Six each side. Three, two, one.”

“Now both legs together — extend, and draw them back to tabletop. Six.”

“Easier: head down, or single leg only — that’s a complete exercise, stay there. More: take the legs lower.”

“Three, two, one. And head down for a breath.”

SCANThe supporting knee drifting towards the chest on the single leg reps — that’s the hip flexor taking over. Cue: ”hold the bent knee still — one leg goes on a journey, one stays home.”

Set 1 — over → under the footbar → hold → legs only → Scissors → return · ~1.75 min

Manual: Abdominal Curls, p.155 (single / double leg extension, holds and scissors variations)

DOOne long set. Tell the room at the start that it’s one set with layers, so nobody thinks they’ve missed a rest.

“This is one set. We build it, we hold it, and then we come out of it.”

“Legs to tabletop. Exhale — curl up, and extend both legs OVER the footbar. The bar is your target. Inhale — draw them back to tabletop.”

“Over the bar is higher, and kinder on the low back. Four.”

“Now same curl — extend UNDER the footbar. Lower legs, harder work. Four.”

“Under the bar only if the spine stays neutral. If it can’t, stay over the bar — or work in imprint. That’s the right answer, not the easy one.”

“Four. Three, two, one — and on this last one, extend and STAY.”

DOHold. Count it once, not the whole way.

“Hold. Arms reaching long towards the footbar, curl lifted from the abdominals. Breathing.”

“Now the legs only — bend the knees to tabletop, extend them long again. Four. The arms stay where they are, the curl stays where it is.”

“Legs long again — and now scissor. One leg reaches ALL the way up towards the ceiling, the other reaches ALL the way down. And switch. Full range, both directions.”

“The legs move. The pelvis stays neutral.”

“Easier: smaller scissor, or head down. More: slow it right down.”

“Four each side. Three, two, one. Both legs together — bend the knees to tabletop, and head down. That’s the set.”

SCANThe low back arching off the carriage on the under-the-bar reps and on the full scissor. Send that person back over the bar, by name. Cue: ”ribs wrapped in and down — only the legs travel.”

Set 2 — extend → hold → Tricep Press → hold → Flutter Kick → return · ~1.75 min

Manual: Abdominal Curls, p.155 (holds variation) · Hands in Straps – Tricep Press, elbow extension

DOSame shape as Set 1 — curl, extend, hold. This time the layer happens at the arms, not the hips.

“Same start. Legs to tabletop, exhale — curl up, and extend both legs long.”

“And hold. Arms long, legs long, curl lifted.”

“Staying exactly there — bend the elbows, and press them straight again. The upper arms don’t move, only the forearms. Six.”

“Elbows point towards the footbar, not out to the sides. Feel the back of the arm.”

“Six. Three, two, one. Arms long again — and hold.”

“Now flutter the legs — small, quick, one up one down. Legs stay long. Twenty beats.”

“Small and quick. The pelvis stays still — the flutter is at the hips, not in the low back.”

“Easier: head down, legs higher, or feet down on the carriage for the tricep press. More: legs lower, and slow the tricep press down.”

“Twenty. Three, two, one. Bend the knees to tabletop, arms above the shoulders, head down. That’s the set.”

SCANShoulders creeping up to the ears during the tricep press, and the low back arching on the flutter. Cue: ”shoulders heavy, upper arms still” and ”flutter smaller — keep the back long on the carriage.”

The Hundred — 100 beats · ~1 min

Manual: Abdominal Curls, p.155 (Hundreds variation)

DOStraight in from the flutter kick — no rest between. Same curl, same long arms, the beat just gets bigger.

“Stay curled, arms long by your sides, legs where you need them. Tabletop, or extended if you want the work.”

“Easier and completely fine: head and shoulders down, legs at tabletop.”

“Pump the arms. Five beats in… two, three, four, five. Five beats out… two, three, four, five.”

“Lift from the abdominals, not the neck. Gaze over the knees.”

DOCount the tens, not the beats. Out loud: “That’s twenty… that’s fifty… that’s eighty.”

“Last twenty. Ribs wrapped in, space between chin and chest. Ten more… and rest everything down.”

ASSESSEDBreath. This is where the principle is most visibly taught — say it plainly: ”five in, five out — that’s the breath the whole class runs on.”
DONO SPRING CHANGE. NO BAR CHANGE. Say so out loud so nobody reaches for a spring out of habit.

“Roll up to sitting and step off to the side for me. Same spring — one red. Bar stays up. Nothing to change.”

DOPre-cue what’s coming.

“We’re going to the legs now — all four exercises on one side, then we leave it. You’ll get the second side later in class, so don’t rush this one. Bring your ball with you.”

14:30 — SECTION 2a · LEGS, FIRST SIDE (5.5 min)

🔴 R · footbar up

Purpose: peak energy. This is the loudest, warmest block of the class — humour belongs here. One red is the lighter, less stable setting — the carriage is looser, so the standing work is wobblier and asks more of the balance and the deep core. Regress with range, or by putting the ball down and holding the footbar — never by adding spring (that would cost two more changes). Four exercises, 5.5 minutes — the extra time goes into longer sets and the manual’s pulses and holds, not more exercises.

DOThe ball stays in the hands through the Long Lunge and the Scooter — it comes down before the Flying Lunge. Pregnancy flag: Flying Lunge foot stays grounded.

Long Lunge + ball pass — 8 + 8 with the reach + 6 passes + 8 in the hold · ~2 min

Manual: Long Lunge, p.125 (pulses and holds variation)

DODemonstrate the ball pass fully — one of your three demos for the whole class. The rhythm of “1 lunge, 1 pass, return” has to be clear before anyone starts.

“Front foot on the floor next to the carriage, just behind the footbar. Back foot against the closest shoulder block. Ball in your hands. Stand tall.”

“Exhale — bend the front knee and press the carriage back, back leg long, body inclining forward. Inhale — straighten the front knee, draw the carriage in and grow tall.”

“Ball stays at your chest for these — no reach yet. Just find the lunge. Eight. Three, two, one.”

“Now the arms come with it. Same lunge — and as the carriage presses back, reach the ball up above your head. Inhale — carriage home, ball back to your chest.”

“Ribs stay down as the arms go up. Eight. Three, two, one.”

“Now the pass. One lunge — press the carriage away. One pass — the ball goes under the front thigh, hand to hand — and return. Lunge… pass… return.”

“A diagonal line from your head to your back heel. Back knee stays off the carriage. Hip bones square to the front.”

“Six. Three, two, one.”

“Now stay down in the lunge and hold it. Only the ball moves — under the thigh, and back over. Eight passes.”

“Easier: shorter range — back knee can rest on the carriage, or hold the ball still. Eight, seven, six, five, four, three, two, one. Carriage home.”

SCANFront knee rolling in or shooting past the ankle. Cue: ”knee over the middle toes, stacked over the ankle.” And sitting into the standing hip — ”don’t sit into that hip.”

Scooter — 8 + 8 heel raise + 8 hold · ~1.25 min

Manual: Scooter, p.124 (hands off the bar = balance challenge; pulses and holds variation)

“Standing foot on the floor next to the carriage, just behind the footbar. Other foot against the closest shoulder rest. Ball held at your chest.”

“Bend through both knees and incline the body forward. Long spine, pelvis square.”

“Exhale — extend the back leg and press the carriage back. Inhale — bend the knee and bring it home. Back knee stays off the carriage.”

“Keep your weight back in the standing heel. Standing knee stacked over the ankle, pressing slightly out towards the little toe.”

“You’ll feel this in the side of the standing glute. Need the bar? Ball down, hands on — that’s fine.”

“Eight. Three, two, one — carriage home.”

“Now add a heel raise. Press back — lift the standing heel — lower it — bring it home. One heel raise every rep. Eight.”

“Easier: keep the heel down, just keep pressing. Three, two, one.”

“Press back and stay there, back leg long. Now just the heel — lift and lower. Carriage stays still, pelvis square. Eight, seven, six, five, four, three, two, one.”

“And carriage home.”

SCANThe standing knee rolling in, and the pelvis. Hands-on moment: one flat hand on each hip, ”hip bones square.” Highest-value adjustment in the class.

Flying Lunge — knee tuck — 10 · ~1 min

Manual: Flying Lunge, p.129 · knee tuck is the leg layer variation

DODemonstrate this fully. The orientation is genuinely confusing from the floor and a demo saves you forty seconds of words.

“Ball down — set it beside the carriage. Hands on the footbar, shoulder-width. Front foot on the floor next to the carriage, back foot against the shoulder rest. Incline forward, spine long.”

“Inhale — bend the front knee and push the carriage away. Exhale — press into your hands and sweep the front leg long behind you — a one-legged plank.”

“Now the layer: draw that flying knee in under you, then sweep it long behind again. The core brings the carriage in — not momentum.”

“Start with a small lift and build. Soft elbows. Hip bones square.”

“Easier: keep the foot on the ground. More: lift the flying leg higher.”

“Ten. This is the hardest thing we do today — and you’re doing it.”

“Three, two, one. And place the foot back beside the footbar.”

Flying Lunge — knee left / right / centre — 6 cycles · ~1 min

Manual: Flying Lunge, p.129 (leg variations) · the knee pattern is in-house

“Same shape — the flying knee now travels. Knee to the outside… to the inside… then centre.”

“Outside, inside, centre. That’s one.”

“Six cycles. Slow it down to find the control.”

“Three, two, one. Foot back beside the footbar, and step off the carriage.”

SCANHip squareness as the knee travels. Cue: ”hip bones stay square to the front.” Second-highest-value hands-on moment.
DONO SPRING CHANGE. NO BAR CHANGE. Keep talking — this junction should be invisible.

“Step onto the carriage and kneel — pad under your knees if you want it. Pick up your ball and set it between your knees. Springs stay exactly as they are — one red, bar up. Nothing to change.”

20:00 — SECTION 3 · CORE, PLANK FAMILY (6 min)

🔴 R (carried) · footbar up · ball between the knees

Purpose: five consecutive wrist- and shoulder-loaded exercises. The ball stays between the knees from Down Stretch through the push-up — the squeeze switches on the inner thighs and the deep core and keeps the legs together as one line. It comes out for both Elephants. The block carries the leg spring rather than taking its own — one red makes Long Stretch and Down Stretch harder to control on the way home, and that is the point. Knees down is the first regression for anyone who can’t hold the line.

DOOffer the wrist option BEFORE the first one — not after someone is already uncomfortable. Pregnancy flag: no full plank — Down Stretch instead of Long Stretch, push-ups on hands and knees, Elephant in a neutral spine only.

“Quick word before we start this block: the next five all load the wrists and shoulders. If wrists are unhappy, make fists — or for the plank, come down onto your forearms. Take it now rather than pushing through.”

Down Stretch — 8 · ~1.25 min

Manual: Down Stretch, p.150

“Hands on the footbar, shoulder-width. Feet up against the shoulder blocks, knees down on the carriage, carriage slightly pressed out — half plank. Keep the ball in between your knees.”

“Press the pelvis forward. The challenge here is to open up through the spine — we’re moving into our Down Stretch.”

“Inhale — arms stay straight, exhale - press the shoulders away from the carriage. inhale — pull the triceps back towards the ribs, and open up through the chest.”

“The movement comes from the shoulders only. Hips always stay forward of the knees.”

“Broaden through the chest as the carriage comes in. Straight arms, soft elbows.”

“Easier: a smaller extension — lift the chest less. More: full range, press further away — the lower you go, the harder it is.”

“Eight. Three, two, one. Carriage home.”

SCANThe low back dipping, or hips sitting back behind the knees. Cue: ”long spine and neck — hips forward of the knees.”

Long Stretch — 6 · ~1.25 min

Manual: Long Stretch, p.149

DOStraight out of Down Stretch — no re-set. Hands, feet and ball are already where they need to be. Call the knee lift from your own reformer so the room sees the line.

The transition — Down Stretch → high plank

“Stay exactly where you are — hands stay shoulder-width on the footbar, fingers and thumbs wrapped over the top. Feet stay against the shoulder blocks. Ball stays between the knees.”

“Exhale — lift the knees up off the carriage into a high plank. One long line, head to heels.”

If the assessor asks “why this order?” — three answers:

  1. The legs are split around the plank block so the shoulder girdle gets a real recovery before eight minutes of pulling — and it costs nothing, because everything after 7:30 sits on one 🔴.
  2. The Flying Lunge knee pattern grows out of the knee tuck — same shape, and it adds the transverse and frontal planes with no set-up change.
  3. The ball is one prop that builds — from a pass (coordination) in the warm-up and lunges, to a squeeze (adductors and deep core) in the bridges and plank block, to a squeeze held high between the thighs in the rows, where it’s what keeps the pelvis square through the rotation.
