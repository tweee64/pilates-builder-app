## Sequencing Logic

- The class followed a clear **lying → kneeling/standing → core → glutes →
  cooldown** arc: warm-up footwork and bridge work lying/seated on the
  carriage, moving to a long kneeling/standing block (splits, skater, arm
  series), then into rotational core work, plank, and glute-focused finishers,
  closing with static stretching.
- The long kneeling/standing block doubled as the class's **unilateral arm
  work** (offering, hug-a-tree, triceps extension) — arm and lower-body/balance
  challenges were layered into the same block rather than isolated, which
  matches the manual's "weave muscle groups throughout" guidance (§5.3) rather
  than a strict single-focus block.
- **Layering (base → challenge)** was used repeatedly and explicitly: the
  instructor offered a lower/easier hand position as a base and a "big set"
  or higher-effort variation ("if you want to challenge yourself…") for the
  same move, e.g. during the squeeze/glute exercise and the plank series.
- **Alignment cues recurred as a throughline**, not just in the warm-up:
  equal space side-to-side, spine straight, "carriage stays still" during arm
  and twist work, hips level/aligned during knee-lift work.

## Cueing Style

- **Setup cues:** specific joint/limb placement before each move — "ball
  behind the left feet," "only the right heel is on," "ball between knees,"
  "hand in the strap."
- **Alignment/correction cues:** repeated emphasis on symmetry and stillness
  — "equal space left and right," "spine straight," "carriage does not come
  straight" (correcting an uneven push), "you are not locking your knees,"
  "keep the carriage still."
- **Breath cues:** explicit exhale-on-effort cueing during the bridge/curl-up
  work ("when you exhale, then you get up").
- **Encouragement/tone:** frequent verbal encouragement ("looking good,
  everyone"), plus a countdown style for nearly every set ("4, 3, 2, and
  last one" / "5, 4, 3, 2, hold").
- **Imagery cues:** used to sharpen effort quality — e.g. a "$10-thousand
  bill between your butt cheeks" for a glute squeeze, "shine like a porch
  light" for a leg lift, and an imagined wall to cap range of motion on an arm
  raise.
- **Individual corrections:** instructor moved between participants during
  the standing/kneeling series with hands-on or verbal 1:1 corrections
  (mentioned adjusting individuals mid-set), consistent with a small mixed
  group.

## Comparison to Standard Framework

Referencing the manual's 45-minute segment framework (§5.3: warm-up 5–7 min,
core 10 min, standing lower body 10 min, lying lower body 7 min, upper body
7 min, cooldown 3–5 min) and spring-change guideline (§5.2, max 3 per class):

- **Warm-up ran long:** ~15 minutes observed vs. the framework's 5–7 minutes.
  This pushed a good amount of lying lower-body and core work (footwork,
  princess bridge, table-top curl-up, straight-leg extension) into the
  "warm-up" label rather than a separate segment — net effect is similar
  total lying-work volume, just organized under one long opening block
  instead of split warm-up + lying-lower-body segments.
- **Spring changes:** 1 change observed, comfortably under the max-3
  guideline — a smoothly run class by this metric.
- **Muscle-group weaving:** matched the manual's guidance well — the
  standing/kneeling block wove arm work (offering, hug-a-tree, triceps) in
  with lower-body/balance challenges (splits, skater) rather than treating
  upper body as an isolated block.
- **Cooldown placement:** static stretching (child's-pose-style release,
  final shoulder/diagonal stretch) was correctly reserved for the very end
  of class, not the warm-up — matches the manual's rule that static
  stretches belong in the cooldown only.
- **Segment order:** deviated somewhat from the framework's suggested order
  (core → standing lower body → lying lower body → upper body) by front-
  loading lying lower-body/core work into an extended warm-up and running the
  arm series and standing lower-body work together mid-class, with a
  dedicated core/rotation and glute block near the end rather than a discrete
  "core" segment near the start.

## Personal Reflection

- The consistent use of **imagery cues** ("$10,000 bill," "porch light")
  stood out as an effective way to communicate a subtle muscular target (deep
  glute squeeze, controlled leg lift) without over-explaining anatomy —
  worth borrowing for cueing similar exercises.
- Offering a **base option alongside a named "challenge"/"big set"** version
  of the same move, rather than just correcting people out of a harder
  variation, seemed to keep the whole mixed-level room moving together and
  matches the manual's "layer up, not down" principle.
- The **long, front-loaded warm-up** is worth noting as a deliberate choice
  rather than a mistake — it may reflect this instructor's preference for
  settling the group into the lying/foundational work before moving to
  higher-balance-demand standing and kneeling exercises later in class.
