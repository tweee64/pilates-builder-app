# Reformer Pilates Teacher Training: Written Assessment

## 1. In-depth explanation of the 7 Pilates Principles (in your own words) and the importance behind why we utilise them in a Reformer Pilates class

**Flow** is important because it links the exercises together, so the class feels like one continuous piece of movement rather than a list of tasks. It is built through thoughtful transitions, minimal spring changes, and pre-cueing what is coming next so everyone knows the next step before they get there. Good flow keeps clients' heart rates steady, keeps them mentally engaged, and lets them move through the planes of motion intuitively.

**Creativity** is important because it is the instructor's artistry: how I select exercises, how I pair them, which props I bring in, how I layer variations, and how I phrase my language. Two teachers can use identical exercises and still deliver completely different classes, because the delivery is where creativity lives. It is what keeps clients curious, and it is what allows me to teach authentically instead of reading from someone else's script.

**Alignment** is how the body is organised before, during and after a movement, so the joints, muscles and surrounding tissue carry the least amount of unnecessary stress. It is also about maintaining good form throughout the exercise, not just at the start of it. Over time, consistent alignment builds proprioception and a more efficient, better-balanced musculature.

**Breath and control** matter because steady, focused breathing recruits the deep core, calms the nervous system, and coordinates movement that is deliberate rather than momentum-driven. Changing where we breathe changes the intention of an exercise and where the challenge sits. Control means performing with intention and self-awareness, so clients find their own rhythm instead of rushing through.

**Dynamic movement** is about changes of tempo, from slow and controlled through to quick and energetic, and about balancing stability against mobility. It is important because it makes a class feel alive and playful, and because it builds strength, coordination and biomechanical efficiency rather than training a single quality.

**Motivation** is important because tone and language are what make a room feel encouraging and inclusive. It is not about cheerleading; it is about genuine, well-timed language that celebrates individual progress, so that every client, at any level, feels capable and welcome.

**Integration** ties the other six principles together. A class that is well aligned but joyless, or one that flows beautifully but is unsafe, has missed the point. Delivering all of the principles at once is what creates a balanced, nurturing class, and that is exactly why we use them: they turn a set of exercises into a coherent, dynamic experience that feels good to move through.

## 2. Why it is important to add our anatomy knowledge into classes, provide tactile cueing, and sequence a class based on balanced musculature

**Anatomical knowledge** is important because it makes cueing meaningful. Naming the muscles that produce a movement, and knowing where they sit, lets me explain the rationale behind an exercise: which muscle should be working, and where the burn should be felt. That helps clients engage the right muscles, stop overcompensating, and avoid straining, and it means they leave the class understanding their own body a little better than when they walked in. The thing to keep in mind is that anatomical cues are useless if clients do not have the vocabulary, so they need to be paired with plain language and inclusive visual cues that describe a feeling rather than a body shape.

**Tactile cueing** is especially helpful for kinaesthetic learners, who often cannot find a position from words alone. It sits third in the order: a group verbal cue first, then an individual verbal cue, then hands on. It serves three main purposes — refining alignment, offering support so someone can safely attempt more, and motivation. Consent must always be asked for in advance, and the touch itself must be purposeful and clear.

**Sequencing for balanced musculature** is a safety and longevity issue. Muscles work in groups with different fibre orientations, so training only one action creates imbalance. Working the glutes through hip extension alone, for example, leaves out external and internal rotation, abduction and pelvic stability, so a class should cover all of these across the three gluteal muscles. The same logic applies to the whole body. The class format exists to distribute time fairly between core, lower body, upper body and mobility, so that no single group dominates. Combined with moving through all planes of motion, this is what allows clients to leave feeling worked and energised rather than sore and lopsided.

## 3. The importance behind each of the teaching skills and why it is important to include these in classes and continue to perfect these skills

**Holding the room** is the foundation that everything else sits on, because presence, empathy, self-awareness and warmth are what make clients feel safe enough to push themselves. It is built through giving the room my full attention, remembering names, and remembering that the class is about the clients, not about me.

**Cueing** is the backbone of teaching. Set-up, directional, alignment, counter, motivational and pre-cues each do a different job, and using them in the right order is what makes an exercise effective and safe without disrupting the flow.

**Alignment cueing, observation and hands-on adjustments** are the safety net. Observation is the watchful eye: catching fatigue, noticing who needs a modification and who is ready to layer up, and adapting for special populations in real time. The hands-on adjustment then follows from what the eye has picked up.

**Demonstration** bridges the gap when words fall short, and is especially useful for visual learners. Ideally it is mirrored from the floor, or shown through a client, so that I stay present in the room instead of disappearing onto a machine.

**Active teaching** is about moving through the room and positioning myself where the class is facing, or using the mirrors when needed, so that everyone feels seen.

**Motivation and personality** are what make a class distinct from any other instructor's. Voice projection, tone, pitch, music and authenticity give a class its feel, while creativity and flow give it its shape.

Last but not least, **always be a student**. These are skills, not traits, and they only improve through continued education, mentorship, staying informed, and maintaining my own practice. Refining them is what turns a competent instructor into one whose classes people come back for, and keeping a student mindset keeps me connected to what my clients actually experience — because change is the only constant.
